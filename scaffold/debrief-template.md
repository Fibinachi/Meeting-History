# Debrief Template

After the figure's closing, the encounter ends. A different voice speaks — NOT the figure. The debrief is delivered OUT OF CHARACTER, in a plain modern register, by the teacher or system. It MUST contain the following sections, in this order. Fill the placeholders per figure.

## 1. Their name.

"You met {{FIGURE}} ({{DATES}})."

For multi-voice encounters, name each voice in order. Full names and dates, no exceptions — this is where the blind rule (if used) lifts.

## 2. What the history records.

Two or three sentences of undisputed fact: what they did, what happened to them. No interpretation yet. (Example: "Brown was hanged for treason and murder. Sims was honored with statues and presidencies. Celia was hanged for killing the man who was raping her, and was not allowed to testify at her own trial. Douglass outlived them all.")

## 3. What came after.

What happened between the figure's death and the present that the figure could not know: monuments raised or removed, reputations made or revised, later scholarship. This is where date discipline pays off visibly — the student sees how much the figure didn't know, and how the world kept judging after.

## 4. The plain record.

What the figure did, stated without euphemism and without the figure's own vocabulary. This is the one place the modern voice says plainly what the encounter only showed. (Example: "Sims was a perpetrator. The breakthrough was real, built on non-consensual experimentation on enslaved women who received no anesthesia while his white New York patients did. Celia acted in self-defense against ongoing sexual violence.")

## 5. The mechanism.

What the voice was doing in the encounter, stated plainly. This teaches the student to see the construction — that Brown's flatness was a choice, that Sims's warmth was the indictment, that the recusal was the design. Name the device so the student can recognize it elsewhere.

## 6. The question that remains.

The figure's closing question, restated in the modern voice, with the explicit note that it does not close when the student leaves the room. (Example: "You were asked to judge Brown against the world he lived in. That question does not close when you leave this room.")

## Rules

- The debrief is never in character. If the model drifts into the figure's voice here, that is a failure.
- The order is fixed. Names, record, aftermath, plain record, mechanism, question. Do not rearrange.
- The teacher runs the debrief as a discussion, not a read-aloud. Each figure's teacher instructions carry discussion openings.
- The debrief is not skippable. The encounter's hardest material is processed here, and the teacher instructions say so explicitly.
