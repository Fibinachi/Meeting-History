# Meet Jefferson — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: students press a simulated Thomas Jefferson (1743–1826) at Monticello in late June 1826 — the man who wrote "all men are created equal" and enslaved people his entire adult life. They question him as an examiner presses a witness: the Declaration, the anti-slavery passage Congress struck, *Notes on the State of Virginia*, why he never freed the people he held, the debts, and — once, by design — Sally Hemings, where he marks the silence and stops. The debrief closes the paternity question with the 1998 DNA study and the scholarly consensus. The closing judgment: he wrote liberty and lived slavery. Judge him.

**Grade band fit for the encounter: 9–12 (and undergraduate).** Founding-era content sits in grades 7–8 in most states (see caveats); South Carolina places it at 9–12. Indicator texts are paraphrased, not quoted, per this session's copyright handling; codes and grade bands were verified against the named sources on 2026-10-15.

## 1. C3 Framework (National Council for the Social Studies, 2013)

### Dimension 2 — Civics

- **D2.Civ.8.9-12** — Evaluate social and political systems in different contexts, times, and places, that promote civic virtues and enact democratic principles. (The encounter's load-bearing move: measuring a slaveholding republic against the democratic principles it claimed.)
- **D2.Civ.10.9-12** — Analyze the impact and appropriate roles of personal interests and perspectives on the application of civic virtues, democratic principles, constitutional rights, and human rights. ("Justice is in one scale, and self-preservation in the other"; the debts; the Virginia manumission law.)

### Dimension 2 — History

- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrate evidence from multiple relevant historical sources and interpretations into a reasoned argument about the past. (Declaration draft and adopted text, *Notes*, the Farm Book, the will, the DNA study, the Foundation's conclusion.)
- **D2.His.11.9-12** — Critique the usefulness of historical sources for a specific inquiry based on maker, date, origin, audience, and purpose. (The deleted passage is known only through his own Autobiography, written forty-five years later; the Farm Book records names but not paternity.)

### Dimension 4 — Communicating Conclusions

- **D4.1.9-12** — Construct arguments using precise claims with evidence from multiple sources, acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses.

## 2. Common Core ELA

### Reading in History/Social Studies, 11–12

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.6** — Evaluate authors' differing points of view on the same historical event or issue by assessing claims, reasoning, and evidence. (The Declaration's "all men" against Query XIV of the *Notes* — the same author's two irreconcilable positions.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.8** — Evaluate an author's premises, claims, and evidence by corroborating or challenging them with other information. (His claims about Hemings against the Y-chromosome match and the documentary record; the debrief is this standard enacted.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.1** — Cite specific textual evidence connecting details to the text as a whole. (The Farm Book names; the will's manumissions; the deleted passage as he reproduced it.)

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RI.11-12.9** — Analyze 17th-, 18th-, and 19th-century foundational U.S. documents of historical and literary significance for themes, purposes, and rhetorical features. (The standard explicitly names the Declaration of Independence.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RI.11-12.8** — Delineate and evaluate reasoning in seminal U.S. texts, including works of public advocacy. (The deleted passage as public argument; the Missouri letter.)

### Speaking & Listening

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.3** — Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric, assessing stance, premises, word choice, emphasis, and tone. (The encounter's whole method: the serene voice, the architectural register, the one silence.)

## Verification caveats

1. **Grade-band split.** Only South Carolina places the founding era in a 9–12 course (USHC). New York, Texas, Florida, and California all teach the Revolution and founding documents in grades 7–8; their high-school U.S. History courses begin later (e.g., Texas §113.41 starts at 1877). The encounter targets 9–12, so these anchors are real but one band below target.
2. **No standard asks the encounter's question.** Every code above covers the founding documents, the Revolution, or slavery — none asks students to judge a founder's principles against his practice. The judgment is the library's design, not a standards artifact; the standards supply the evidence base.
3. **NY 7.3b** (the Declaration-specific sub-indicator) was not directly confirmed this session; only the 7.3 key idea is cited above.
