# Meet Aaron Burr — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: the student questions Burr (1756–1836) as an examiner presses a witness — through the Cooper letter, the disputed morning at Weehawken, the western expedition and Richmond, and Theodosia — separating what is documented, what is disputed, and what is myth. It closes with the verdict: *Murderer? Traitor? Or the most maligned man in American history? Judge him.*

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12** (high school US History / ELA literacy). Note: Burr-era content anchors sit in Grade 8 in Texas, Florida, and California (see caveats); the 9–12 weight comes from the evidence-and-argumentation standards, which are band-matched, plus South Carolina's USHC course.

---

## 1. C3 Framework (National Council for the Social Studies, 2013)

Grade band: **9–12**. (Indicator texts verified against curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim; the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable in this session — see verification note at end.)

The encounter's load-bearing beats are all evidence work: the seconds' disagreeing accounts, Wilkinson's doctored cipher, the missing two witnesses at Richmond, acquittal ≠ innocence.

### Dimension 2 — History

- **D2.His.9.9-12** — Analyze the relationship between historical sources and the secondary interpretations made from them. (Burr's own letters vs. the villain-myth made from them — the encounter's central gap.)
- **D2.His.10.9-12** — Detect possible limitations in various kinds of historical evidence and differing secondary interpretations. (Who fired first is genuinely unresolved: the only witnesses turned their backs and later disagreed.)
- **D2.His.11.9-12** — Critique the usefulness of historical sources for a specific historical inquiry based on their maker, date, place of origin, intended audience, and purpose. (Wilkinson's cipher letter — a source the government itself relied on, manufactured by a man later shown to be on Spain's payroll.)
- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrate evidence from multiple relevant historical sources and interpretations into a reasoned argument about the past. (The closing verdict is argued from the whole record, not from Burr's charm or the books' myth.)

### Dimension 4 — Communicating Conclusions

- **D4.1.9-12** — Construct arguments using precise and knowledgeable claims, with evidence from multiple sources, while acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses. (The verdict must state what the record does *not* support — the expedition's true object was never proven — alongside what it does.)
- **D4.4.9-12** — Critique the use of claims and evidence in arguments for credibility. (Burr defends himself with complete courtesy; the student critiques the advocate's claims the way a juror would.)

*Verified against: nrhp.mnhs.org and curriculum.llilasbenson.utexas.edu primary-source-packet standards documents; c3teachers.org blueprint documents; elmbrookschools.org AP European History curriculum. Official document: NCSS, 2013 (socialstudies.org/c3).*

## 2. Common Core ELA — Literacy in History/Social Studies, Speaking/Listening & Writing

Grade band: **9–12**. (Verified against thecorestandards.org.)

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.6** — Evaluate authors' differing points of view on the same historical event or issue by assessing the authors' claims, reasoning, and evidence. (Burr's account vs. Hamilton's pre-duel statement vs. the seconds vs. Jefferson's camp — four points of view on the same events, each assessed on claims, reasoning, evidence.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.3** — Evaluate various explanations for actions or events and determine which explanation best accords with textual evidence, acknowledging where the text leaves matters uncertain. (The expedition's object; who fired first — the prompt *marks* the uncertainty and forbids Burr from resolving it, which is exactly what this standard trains.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.8** — Evaluate an author's premises, claims, and evidence by corroborating or challenging them with other information. (The student corroborates or challenges Burr's claims against the trial record and the debrief's plain record.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.1** — Initiate and participate effectively in a range of collaborative discussions (one-on-one, in groups, and teacher-led) with diverse partners on grades 11–12 topics, texts, and issues, building on others' ideas and expressing their own clearly and persuasively. (The debrief discussion openings are built to produce disagreement over the verdict.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.W.11-12.1** — Write arguments to support claims in an analysis of substantive topics or texts, using valid reasoning and relevant and sufficient evidence. (The verdict — murderer, traitor, or most maligned — as post-encounter argument.)

*Verified against: https://thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RH/11-12/6/, https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/W/11-12/1/ (RH.11-12.3, RH.11-12.8, and SL.11-12.1 texts cross-confirmed via CCSS-reproducing district documents, consistent with the Trial of John Brown alignment verified the same date).*

## 3. South Carolina — Social Studies College- and Career-Ready Standards (2019), US History and the Constitution

Grade band: **9–12** (high school US History). (Verified against the official SC Department of Education 2019 printable standards.)

- **USHC.1.CC** — Analyze the processes of continuity and change in the debates over the proper role of the central government and neutrality in foreign affairs from 1789–1815. (The Burr years sit squarely inside this indicator's range: the 1800 tie and the rise of party government, the duel's political origins, and the treason trial — the first great federal test of the constitutional definition of treason, with the Chief Justice presiding.)
- **USHC.1.P** — Summarize the changing relationship between individuals and the government during the period 1607–1800. (Burr as the individual who moved through every branch of that relationship — soldier, senator, vice-president, defendant.)
- **Standard 1** (framing) — Demonstrate an understanding of the influence of the Atlantic World on the regional and national development of republicanism and federalism from 1607–1815.

## 4. Other states (quick hits)

Grade band: **9–12** for the skills frameworks; see grade-band caveats on content anchors.

### Texas (TEKS)

- **§113.20(b)(5)(C)** (Social Studies, Grade 8) — Explain the origin and development of American political parties. (The 1800 tie — the electoral mechanism that produced the Burr–Jefferson deadlock and seeded the Hamilton feud — is the case study.) *Grade-band caveat: Texas places all early-republic content in Grade 8 (§113.20); the high-school US History course (§113.41) covers 1877–present and has no Burr-era anchor.*

*Verified against: TEA's official Grade 8 breakout document (tea.texas.gov) for §113.20 structure; the (5)(C) text verified against a district TEKS mirror — recheck against the TEA-hosted document before print use.*

### Florida (NGSSS / State Academic Standards)

- **SS.8.A.3.14** (Grade 8) — Explain major domestic and international economic, military, political, and sociocultural events of Thomas Jefferson's presidency. (The duel (1804) and the treason trial (1807) both occurred during Jefferson's presidency, under his administration's prosecution.) *Grade-band caveat: Florida places early-republic content in Grade 8.*
- **SS.912.A.1.2** (Grades 9–12) — Utilize a variety of primary and secondary sources to identify author, historical significance, audience, and authenticity to understand a historical period. (The source-critique strand of the encounter — Wilkinson's cipher, the seconds' accounts, Burr's letters.)

*SS.8.A.3.14 verified against district pacing guides quoting the CPALMS benchmark text — not directly on cpalms.org. SS.912.A.1.2 verified per the Trial of John Brown alignment (2026-10-15). Codes stable across NGSSS and the 2023 State Academic Standards adoption.*

### California (History–Social Science Content Standards, Grade 8)

- **8.3.4** — Understand how the conflicts between Thomas Jefferson and Alexander Hamilton resulted in the emergence of two political parties (e.g., view of foreign policy, Alien and Sedition Acts, economic policy, National Bank, funding and assumption of the revolutionary debt). (The closest single anchor in the five states: the Jefferson–Hamilton conflict is the engine of Burr's whole story.) *Grade-band caveat: California's high-school HSS course begins with the Civil War era; early-republic content sits in Grade 8.*
- **8.4.1** (Grade 8) — Describe the country's physical landscapes, political divisions, and territorial expansion during the terms of the first four presidents. (The western expedition's setting: the Ohio, the Mississippi, New Orleans, the Spanish borderlands.)

*Verified against: the CA Department of Education History–Social Science Framework, Appendix C (cde.ca.gov).*

### New York (K–12 Social Studies Framework, Grade 11)

- **11.2** — CONSTITUTIONAL FOUNDATIONS (1763–1824): "Growing political and economic tensions led the American colonists to declare their independence from Great Britain. Once independent, the new nation confronted the challenge of creating a stable federal republic." (The duel, the treason trial, and the 1800 election all fall within 1763–1824; 11.2c covers the Constitution, its debates, and compromises.) *Caveat: no NY indicator names Burr; this is the era anchor, honestly labeled.*

## Verification caveats

1. **C3 Framework**: indicator texts were verified against multiple independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (consistent with each other); the official NCSS PDF itself was not directly retrievable this session. Official publisher: NCSS (2013), socialstudies.org/c3.
2. **No standard names Burr** in any of the five states — unlike NY's framework, which names John Brown's raid in 11.3b, Burr has no named-indicator anchor anywhere checked. The content alignments above are era anchors, labeled as such.
3. **Grade-band split**: SC USHC and NY 11.2 anchor the Burr era in grades 9–12; Texas, Florida, and California place all early-republic content in Grade 8 (TX §113.20, FL SS.8.A.3, CA 8.3/8.4). Florida's and California's high-school courses begin with the Civil War era. The 9–12 weight of this encounter therefore rests on the skills standards (RH, SL, W, D2.His/D4) plus SC and NY.
4. **Texas and Florida content texts**: §113.20(b)(5)(C) and SS.8.A.3.14 texts were verified against district mirrors/pacing guides quoting the official benchmarks, not the TEA- or FLDOE-hosted documents — recheck before print use.
