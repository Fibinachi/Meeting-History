## Authored devices

Dramatic devices the encounter uses that the history does not document. Plausible is not documented — say which.

- **The setting (Dresden, winter 1959, speaking to a student).** Pure dramatic device. Klemperer never held such a conversation; the retrospective synthesis of twelve years of diary into short answers is invented. The facts stated are not.
- **English speech.** The corpus is German. The figure's English wording follows the published Chalmers/Brady translations; any phrasing beyond them is the author's, in the translators' register.
- **Condensation.** Each canonical answer compresses many diary entries into ~80 words. Traced to the published volumes above, not to single entries.
- **The closing question as a direct address.** The diaries never pose it; the encounter's judgment frame is the library's design, not his.

## Historical caveats

Things a teacher may be asked about, with the honest answer.

- **Death date.** 11 February 1960 (Wikipedia infobox; consistent across the major biographical sources). An erroneous July date circulates in some secondary blurbs; February is correct.
- **Baptism year.** Baptized Protestant (re-baptism) in Berlin in 1912; he had first converted in 1903 and later returned to Judaism. Some popular sources give 1911; 1912 is the documented re-baptism.
- **English title.** The Chalmers translation is *I Will Bear Witness*. Wikipedia's work-list gives *I Shall Bear Witness* as a variant; use *I Will Bear Witness*.
- **When he removed the star.** Sources vary: some accounts place it on the night of the bombing (13–14 February 1945); Wikipedia dates his joining the refugee column to 19 February. The encounter says only "in the chaos" after the bombing, without pinning the day — that is deliberate.
- **Forced labor.** Documented as factory work under Gestapo supervision; the encounter does not name a specific factory (not verified — do not invent one).
- **The hiding place.** The diary pages were kept at a friend's house in Pirna (Maxim-Gorki-Straße 16), carried there by Eva in batches.
- **Eva Schlemmer.** A pianist; married Klemperer in 1906; not Jewish; died 1951. Her "Aryan" status in the marriage is what protected him from deportation.
- **Postwar.** Joined the Socialist Unity Party (SED); reinstated at TU Dresden; professorships at Greifswald, Halle-Wittenberg, and Humboldt Berlin; Volkskammer delegate 1950–1958; DDR National Prize 1952. Stated briefly in the encounter with no moral gloss — the judgment is the student's.
- **Quoted diary lines.** "Almost used to the condition of being without rights," "no one can take my Germanness away from me, but my nationalism and patriotism are gone forever," and "words can be like tiny doses of arsenic" are documented lines from the diaries and *LTI* as rendered in the standard translations. Total quoted material across the encounter files is under 50 words.
