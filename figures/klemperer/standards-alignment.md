# Meet Victor Klemperer — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: students converse with Victor Klemperer (1881–1960) about the twelve years of the Nazi regime — the diary, the laws closing in year by year, the poisoned words of LTI, his wife Eva, the bombing of Dresden — and at the end render the closing judgment: "He watched, he wrote, he survived. He didn't resist. Judge him."

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12**, except where noted.

---

## 1. State Holocaust-education mandates (the strongest structural fit)

Four of the five states mandate Holocaust instruction by statute; South Carolina does not.

- **Florida** — F.S. **1003.42(2)(g)**: requires public K–12 instruction in "the history of the Holocaust (1933-1945), the systematic, planned annihilation of European Jews and other groups by Nazi Germany," taught so as to lead to "an investigation of human behavior, an understanding of the ramifications of prejudice, racism, and stereotyping." Districts must annually certify compliance; the second week in November is "Holocaust Education Week." *Verified against the 2024 Florida Statutes via flsenate.gov (mandate dates to 1994).*
- **New York** — Education Law **§801**: the Regents "shall prescribe courses of instruction in patriotism, citizenship, and human rights issues, with particular attention to the study of the inhumanity of genocide, slavery… [and] the Holocaust… to be maintained and followed in all the schools of the state." A 2022 law (Ch. 490, sponsored by Sen. Kaplan) requires superintendents to attest that districts teach the Holocaust. NYSED's official guidance places explicit Holocaust instruction in grades 8, 10, and 11. *Verified against nysed.gov and nysenate.gov.*
- **Texas** — Education Code **§29.9072** (SB 1828, 2019): "Holocaust Remembrance Week" — public schools must provide age-appropriate Holocaust instruction each year using materials developed or approved by the Texas Holocaust, Genocide, and Antisemitism Advisory Commission. *Verified against tea.texas.gov and thgaac.texas.gov.*
- **California** — listed among the states with mandatory Holocaust/genocide education (1988 Model Curriculum for Human Rights and Genocide; 2021 "Never Again Education Act," SB 693, establishing the Governor's Council on Genocide and Holocaust Education). *Verified against the AJC "Moving Toward Never Again" report (2022) and the current 23-state mandate list.*
- **South Carolina — no codified mandate.** SC has a Holocaust Council (commission) but no statute requiring Holocaust instruction; instruction is carried through the standards. *Verified against ecs.org and the AJC report. Say this plainly rather than implying a mandate.*

## 2. C3 Framework (NCSS, 2013)

- **D2.His.4.9-12** — Analyze complex and interacting factors that influenced the perspectives of people during different historical eras. (The "noose" sequence: why staying, writing, and not resisting made sense from inside his position.)
- **D2.His.10.9-12** — Detect possible limitations in various kinds of historical evidence and differing secondary interpretations. (A single man's diary: what it can and cannot establish about the camps, the scale, the regime.)
- **D2.His.11.9-12** — Critique the usefulness of historical sources for a specific historical inquiry based on their maker, date, place of origin, intended audience, and purpose. (The diary as source: written day by day, hidden, unpublished in his lifetime.)
- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrate evidence from multiple relevant historical sources and interpretations into a reasoned argument about the past.
- **D4.1.9-12** — Construct arguments using precise and knowledgeable claims, with evidence from multiple sources, while acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses. ("Judge him" demands both sides of the verdict.)

*Indicator texts verified against multiple curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (see the library's Trial of John Brown alignment for the verification set); the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable.*

## 3. Common Core ELA

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.6** — Evaluate authors' differing points of view on the same historical event or issue by assessing the authors' claims, reasoning, and evidence. (Klemperer's account against postwar interpretations of witness, complicity, and "resistance.")
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.3** — Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric, assessing the stance, premises, links among ideas, word choice, points of emphasis, and tone used. (The encounter is an extended oral source to evaluate; his flatness is the thing to notice.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RI.11-12.4** — Determine the meaning of words and phrases as they are used in a text, including figurative, connotative, and technical meanings; analyze how an author uses and refines the meaning of a key term or terms over the course of a text. (The LTI thread: *fanatisch*, *total*, *Volk*, *gleichschalten* — words whose value changed by repetition.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.WHST.11-12.1** — Write arguments focused on discipline-specific content. (If the closing judgment is assigned in writing.)

*RH.11-12.6, SL.11-12.3, and WHST.11-12.1 texts verified against thecorestandards.org (the first two verified directly in this library's Trial of John Brown alignment, 2026-10-15); RI.11-12.4 text verified against the CCSS grade-level progression.*

## 5. Other states (quick hits)

### Texas (TEKS, US History Since 1877, §113.41)
- **(c)(7)(C)** — Analyze major issues of World War II, including the Holocaust; the internment of Japanese Americans and Executive Order 9066; and the development of atomic weapons. *Verified against TEA 2023 correspondence and §113.41 text.* (Note: pre-2018 printings letter this indicator (7)(D); the current streamlined TEKS use (C).)

### Florida (State Academic Standards, 9–12)
- **SS.912.A.6.3** — Analyze the impact of the Holocaust during World War II on Jews as well as other groups. *Verified against flrules.org and the 2023-adoption skill plan.*
- **SS.912.W.7.8** — Explain the causes, events, and effects of the Holocaust (1933-1945) including its roots in the long tradition of antisemitism, 19th century ideas about race and nation, and Nazi dehumanization of the Jews and other victims. (World History.) *Verified against flrules.org and the Florida World History compilation.*

### California (HSS Content Standards)
- **10.8.5** (Grade 10) — Analyze the Nazi policy of pursuing racial purity, especially against the European Jews; its transformation into the Final Solution; and the Holocaust that resulted in the murder of six million Jewish civilians. *Verified against CA HSS compilations quoting the standard.*
- **11.7.5** (Grade 11) — Discuss the constitutional issues and impact of events on the U.S. home front, including… the response of the administration to Hitler's atrocities against Jews and other groups… *Verified against CA HSS compilations.*

### New York (K–12 Social Studies Framework, Grade 11)
- **11.8c** — In response to World War II and the Holocaust, the United States played a major role in efforts to prevent such human suffering in the future. (Performance indicators: American officials' knowledge of the Holocaust and whether intervention was possible; Nuremberg; the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.) *Verified against the official framework PDF (2017 revision), via archived mirror.*

## Verification caveats

1. **C3 Framework**: indicator texts verified against three independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim; the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable. Official publisher: NCSS (2013), socialstudies.org/c3.
2. **South Carolina mandate**: SC has no codified Holocaust-education mandate — only a council and standards anchors. Do not present it as a mandate state.
3. **Texas lettering**: §113.41(c)(7)'s Holocaust indicator is (C) in the current streamlined TEKS and (D) in pre-2018 printings; the quoted text is stable across both.
4. **California mandate**: CA's mandate status is verified from the AJC 2022 mandate report and the current 23-state list; the operative classroom standards are 10.8.5 and 11.7.5 as cited.
