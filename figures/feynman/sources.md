## Authored devices

Dramatic devices the encounter uses that the history does not document. Plausible is not documented — say which.

- **The conversation itself and its setting.** Feynman never met 2026 students. The encounter premise — his Caltech office, early 1988, the blackboard behind him — is a pure dramatic device. The blackboard's contents are documented by the Caltech Archives photograph; the conversation is not.
- **Compression.** Stories from 1943–1988 are told as one sitting. That is the encounter form, not the history.
- **The TO LEARN list is kept vague.** The prompt has him gesture at "TO LEARN" without naming specific items, because identifications of the list's contents (Bethe Ansatz, Kondo effect, etc.) come from secondary readings of the archive photograph, not from his own statement.
- **The Arline letter is deliberately excluded.** It is in the corpus (the letters collection) but OUT of the encounter: the letter was sealed, written in 1946, and found after his death — he does not know it survived. Date discipline forbids him mentioning it. It appears in teacher instructions and the debrief's "what came after," never in his voice.

## Historical caveats

Things a teacher may be asked about, with the honest answer. Written before the figure ships, even the ones decided not to matter.

- **The memoirs are anecdotal.** Both books were dictated to Ralph Leighton late in Feynman's life, built from stories he had been telling — and polishing — for decades. Treat them as his telling, not sworn fact. The prompt's guardrails forbid inventing new anecdotes in his style; he may tell only corpus stories, and deflects the rest ("That sounds like a good story. It's not mine.").
- **He did not solve Challenger alone.** Morton Thiokol engineers Roger Boisjoly and Allan McDonald had warned against a cold-weather launch before the disaster; within the commission, Sally Ride supplied Feynman with key information. His ice-water demonstration dramatized a known failure mode on television — it did not discover it. The prompt keeps the demo minimal (a piece of O-ring material, ice water, loss of resilience, on camera) and does not claim sole credit.
- **The blackboard line is posthumous documentation, not a published doctrine.** "What I cannot create, I do not understand" is known from the Caltech Archives photograph taken after his death — not from a book or lecture. Its meaning in the encounter (rebuild it yourself; the Lectures method) is the honest reading, not a quotation with a citation.
- **"If you can't explain it to a freshman…" is unattested.** Widely attributed to Feynman; nowhere in the corpus. The prompt has him disown it honestly rather than canonize it.
- **Tuva timing is uncertain.** Soviet permission to visit arrived after his death; accounts differ on exactly when (the day after vs. about a week later). He never went. State it vaguely or not at all.
- **The Nobel was shared.** 1965, with Julian Schwinger and Sin-Itiro Tomonaga; Freeman Dyson showed the three formulations of QED equivalent. The encounter's opening says "shared the Nobel Prize" — keep it that way.
- **The depression telling is late-life.** His account of falling into a depressive state after Trinity and losing Arline comes from interviews near the end of his life (e.g., the 1981 BBC interview and later archive footage), not from contemporary 1945 letters — in which he sounds elated and proud. Both are his voice at different times; the encounter uses the later telling for the Los Alamos beat and marks the difference here.
- **Arline's death.** Arline Greenbaum Feynman died of tuberculosis in June 1945, weeks before the Trinity test (July 16, 1945). He drove from Los Alamos to be with her.
