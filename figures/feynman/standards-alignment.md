# Meet Richard Feynman — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: students question Feynman on QED and the path-integral pictures, his teaching method, Los Alamos, and the Challenger O-ring demonstration. Closing judgment: he said the pleasure was in figuring things out — did he live it?

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12** (high school physics/ELA literacy; delivery is intelligible to younger students, but the judgment — the bomb — belongs in high school).

---

## 1. NGSS — Science and Engineering Practices + Nature of Science (NGSS 2013)

*Honesty note up front: no high-school disciplinary core idea covers quantum electrodynamics or path integrals — QED sits outside the NGSS DCI scope. The NGSS alignment for this figure is carried entirely by the practices and the nature of science, which is exactly what the encounter is about.*

### Science and Engineering Practices (NGSS Appendix F)

- **Practice 7: Engaging in Argument from Evidence.** The 9–12 elements include: evaluate the claims, evidence, and/or reasoning behind currently accepted explanations or solutions to determine the merits of arguments (7.2); and construct, use, and/or present an oral and written argument or counter-arguments based on data and evidence (7.4). The Challenger thread is the exemplar: Feynman tested a prediction (cold O-ring rubber loses its seal) against the managers' procedural and probabilistic claims, and presented the evidence on television — argument from evidence defeating argument from authority.
- **Practice 3: Planning and Carrying Out Investigations.** The ice-water O-ring test is the classroom exemplar: a simple, decisive investigation designed to produce data that serve as the basis for evidence. (Practice title verified against Appendix F; element-level wording for 9–12 not re-verified this session.)
- **Practice 2: Developing and Using Models.** The diagrams are bookkeeping models — "a picture for each way the thing can happen, so you don't lose track" — which became the working language of particle physics. (Practice title verified; cited at name level.)

*These practices are embedded in every state's NGSS-based standards (CA, NY, SC 2021) with no separate state code — they are cited here once under NGSS rather than repeated per state.*

### Nature of science (NGSS Appendix H)

- **AH.2** — Scientific knowledge is based on empirical evidence. ("Nature doesn't care about your schedule. Nature cannot be fooled." The ice-water demo is AH.2 in miniature.)
- **AH.7** — Science is a human endeavor. (Los Alamos: "you stop thinking" — the social conditions of knowledge production, and what they cost.)
- **AH.1** — Scientific investigations use a variety of methods. (The wobbling-plate curiosity, the safe-cracking, the commission testimony — methods ranging from play to forensics.)

*Verified against: NGSS Appendix F via the official-text compilations (Physics Classroom NGSS practices page, commonplanner Appendix F), NGSS Appendix H (official text via standards mirror). Official: NGSS Lead States (2013), nextgenscience.org.*

## 2. C3 Framework (National Council for the Social Studies, 2013)

### Dimension 2 — History

- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrate evidence from multiple relevant historical sources and interpretations into a reasoned argument about the past. (The closing judgment integrates the Nobel, Los Alamos, and Challenger into one verdict.)
- **D2.His.6.9-12** — Analyze the ways in which the perspectives of those writing history shaped the history that they produced. (The teacher instructions flag this explicitly: both memoirs were dictated late in life from anecdotes polished over decades; his 1945 letters sound elated about Trinity while the late-life telling sounds depressed — the same man, two tellings.)
- **D2.His.4.9-12** — Analyze complex and interacting factors that influenced the perspectives of people during different historical eras. (Wartime Los Alamos; the 1986 commission politics.)

### Dimension 4 — Communicating Conclusions and Taking Informed Action

- **D4.1.9-12** — Construct arguments using precise and knowledgeable claims, with evidence from multiple sources, while acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses. (The closing judgment: "did he live it?" — argued against the whole life, the bomb included.)
- **D4.4.9-12** — Critique the use of claims and evidence in arguments for credibility. (The debrief's plain record: the demo dramatized a known failure mode — it did not discover it; hold the charm and the fact together.)

*Verified against: independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (Michigan History Day guide, Minnesota Historical Society, Greenwich Public Schools, National History Day, Truman Library). Official NCSS PDF not directly retrievable this session — publisher NCSS (2013).*

## 3. Common Core ELA — Literacy in Science/History, Speaking/Listening, Writing

### Reading in Science & Technical Subjects, 11–12

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RST.11-12.2** — Determine the central ideas or conclusions of a text; summarize complex concepts, processes, or information presented in a text by paraphrasing them in simpler but still accurate terms. (The path-integral explanation — "it takes every path" — is the encounter's performance of this standard; his teaching philosophy is that understanding *is* this paraphrase.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RST.11-12.8** — Evaluate the hypotheses, data, analysis, and conclusions in a science or technical text, verifying the data when possible and corroborating or challenging conclusions with other sources of information. (The O-ring thread: the managers' claims vs. the rubber in ice water.)

### Reading in History/Social Studies, 11–12

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.6** — Evaluate authors' differing points of view on the same historical event or issue by assessing the authors' claims, reasoning, and evidence. (His 1945 letters vs. his late-life telling of Los Alamos — the anchor already noted in the teacher instructions' condensed alignment.)

### Speaking & Listening

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.3** — Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric, assessing the stance, premises, links among ideas, word choice, points of emphasis, and tone used. (The ice-water demo as rhetoric: plain evidence staged against institutional language.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.1** — Initiate and participate effectively in a range of collaborative discussions, building on others' ideas and expressing their own clearly and persuasively. (The encounter is conversational by design — and he asks questions back.)

### Writing in History/Social Studies, Science, and Technical Subjects

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.WHST.11-12.1** — Write arguments focused on discipline-specific content. (The written closing judgment: did he live it?)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.WHST.11-12.2** — Write informative/explanatory texts to examine and convey complex ideas, concepts, and information clearly and accurately through the effective selection, organization, and analysis of content. (His teaching philosophy as a student performance: explain something hard in plain words with pictures, the way the *Lectures* do.)

*Verified against: NJ Student Learning Standards ELA Companion 11–12 (nj.gov — RST.11-12.2, RST.11-12.8, WHST.11-12.1, WHST.11-12.2 verbatim); thecorestandards.org cross-confirmed via CCSS-reproducing district documents (RH.11-12.6, SL.11-12.1/3).*

## 5. Other states (quick hits)

### Texas (TEKS, Science)

- **§112.45 Physics, adopted 2020 — P.8.A**: describe the photoelectric effect and the dual nature of light. (The wave–particle duality that QED supersedes and Feynman's diagrams describe. QED itself is beyond the HS band — the anchor is the duality content, cited honestly.)
- **§112.45 Physics, adopted 2020 — (c)(1)–(c)(3), scientific processes**: analyze, evaluate, and critique scientific explanations using empirical evidence and logical reasoning; communicate scientific information. (The practices strand behind the Challenger thread.)

*Verified against: TEA 19 TAC Chapter 112 section list (tea.texas.gov); EISD physics unit guide (P.8A). Caveat: the P.8.A sub-code placement was verified against a district TEKS curriculum resource reflecting the 2020 TEKS, not the official TAC text.*

## Verification caveats

1. **C3 Framework**: indicator texts verified against five independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (all agree); the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable this session. Publisher: NCSS (2013).
2. **No DCI coverage**: stated plainly in section 1 — QED and path integrals are outside high-school DCI scope in every jurisdiction checked. The encounter's science value is practices and nature of science, not content coverage; do not present it as teaching QED to the standards.
3. **Practice-element verification**: Practice 7's 9–12 elements were verified against Appendix F compilations; Practices 2 and 3 are cited at the practice-title level, with Practice 3's element wording flagged as not re-verified this session.
4. **No Common Core Math cited**: the encounter's physics is deliberately equation-free ("never hide behind equations"), so no math practice is claimed.
