## Historical caveats

Things a teacher may be asked about, with the honest answer.

- **The "delay" is partly a later framing.** Historian John van Wyhe argues the idea that Darwin *delayed* out of fear dates to the 1940s; his contemporaries thought his pace reasonable — he always finished one book before starting another, told many people about his transmutation work without outrage, and left the 1844 memorandum arranging posthumous publication. The encounter gives **Darwin's own stated reasons** (evidence-gathering, Beagle volumes, barnacles, illness, family, care for friends' and Emma's feelings, wanting to be sure) and refuses to reduce them to one motive; the historiography is noted here for the teacher.
- **The autobiography was censored by his son.** Francis Darwin's 1887 edition removed the religious criticism; Nora Barlow restored it in 1958. Any edition a student finds may differ on exactly these passages.
- **The Lady Hope deathbed-conversion story is apocryphal.** A claim that Darwin recanted to an evangelist in his final days has no evidence; his family denied it at every opportunity.
- **"Survival of the fittest" was Herbert Spencer's phrase**, borrowed by Darwin for the 5th edition of the *Origin* (1869). It was never his coinage and never a social program of his.
- **He had no mechanism of heredity and said so.** Mendel's work was unknown to him. The encounter treats this admitted gap as load-bearing honesty, not a gotcha.
- **Victorian racial vocabulary is in the corpus.** *The Descent of Man* contains racial hierarchies typical of his time and class; the encounter paraphrases around them and never reproduces the slur-adjacent vocabulary. The teacher should know they are there.
- **Wallace was gracious afterward.** He thanked Darwin, praised the decades of research, and later coined "Darwinism" to credit him — documented in the correspondence. The encounter is fair to Wallace by design.
- **Emma Darwin kept her faith to the end of her life** (she died 1896); her side of the religious difference is documented in *Emma Darwin: A Century of Family Letters* (ed. Henrietta Litchfield, 1905/1915).
- **Annie Darwin died April 23, 1851, aged ten.** Darwin's infant son Charles Waring died of scarlet fever days before the July 1, 1858 Linnean Society reading; Darwin was not present. (Family illness at the time included diphtheria in the household.)
