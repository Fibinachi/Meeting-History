# Meet Darwin — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: students question Charles Darwin (winter 1881) — the Beagle, the barnacles, what natural selection means in his own words, the twenty-year delay, Wallace and 1858, his faith and Annie, Emma — and at the end render one judgment: he knew what his idea would do to his world, and he published it anyway. Judge the man.

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12** (high school biology / ELA literacy); also fit for undergraduate introductory courses.

---

## 1. C3 Framework (National Council for the Social Studies, 2013)

Grade band: **9–12**. (Indicator texts verified against independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim; the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable — see caveats.)

### Dimension 2 — History

- **D2.His.2.9-12** — Analyze change and continuity in historical eras. *(The world before and after November 1859; the twenty-year delay as continuity against the publication as rupture.)*
- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrate evidence from multiple relevant historical sources and interpretations into a reasoned argument about the past. *(The judgment requires integrating the autobiography, the correspondence, the Wallace letters, and the Origin.)*

### Dimension 4 — Communicating Conclusions and Taking Informed Action

- **D4.1.9-12** — Construct arguments using precise and knowledgeable claims, with evidence from multiple sources, while acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses. *(The encounter's honesty question: is admitting the missing mechanism of heredity a weakness or the proof of honesty?)*
- **D4.4.9-12** — Critique the use of claims and evidence in arguments for credibility.

## 2. Common Core ELA — Science & Technical Subjects and Speaking/Listening

Grade band: **9–12**. (Verified against thecorestandards.org. The 9–10 band versions are exercised identically.)

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RST.11-12.2** — Determine the central ideas or conclusions of a text; summarize complex concepts, processes, or information presented in a text by paraphrasing them in simpler but still accurate terms. *(He explains natural selection through the pigeon fanciers and Malthus; students must restate the mechanism accurately.)*
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RST.11-12.8** — Evaluate the hypotheses, data, analysis, and conclusions in a science or technical text, verifying the data when possible and corroborating or challenging conclusions with other sources of information. *(The eight barnacle years as evidence; the admission in print that the laws of inheritance were unknown to him.)*
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.3** — Evaluate various explanations for actions or events and determine which explanation best accords with textual evidence, acknowledging where the text leaves matters uncertain. *(The competing accounts of the twenty-year delay: the evidence, the health, Emma, fear of the reception.)*
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.3** — Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric, assessing the stance, premises, links among ideas, word choice, points of emphasis, and tone used. *(The device: hedged sentences landing certain on science, honestly uncertain on religion — students must hear where the hedging stops.)*
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.4** — Present information, findings, and supporting evidence, conveying a clear and distinct perspective, such that listeners can follow the line of reasoning, alternative or opposing perspectives are addressed, and the organization, development, substance, and style are appropriate to purpose, audience, and a range of formal and informal tasks. *(Students present their closing judgment in the debrief.)*
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.W.11-12.1** — Write arguments to support claims in an analysis of substantive topics or texts, using valid reasoning and relevant and sufficient evidence. *(The closing judgment, rendered in writing.)*

## 3. NGSS (Next Generation Science Standards, 2013)

Grade band: **9–12**. (Performance expectations verified via NSTA and AMNH NGSS card-deck reproductions; Appendix H understandings via the official Appendix H text.)

### Disciplinary Core Ideas — the encounter's science, as he states it

- **HS-LS4-1** — Communicate scientific information that common ancestry and biological evolution are supported by multiple lines of empirical evidence. *(His "descent with modification"; the fossil record, the barnacles, the breeders.)*
- **HS-LS4-2** — Construct an explanation based on evidence that the process of evolution primarily results from four factors: (1) the potential for a species to increase in number, (2) the heritable genetic variation of individuals in a species due to mutation and sexual reproduction, (3) competition for limited resources, and (4) the proliferation of those organisms that are better able to survive and reproduce in the environment. *(He states these himself: Malthus's arithmetic, variation, the struggle for existence.)*
- **HS-LS4-4** — Construct an explanation based on evidence for how natural selection leads to adaptation of populations. *(Natural selection as he means it: of beetles and barnacles.)*

### Nature of Science (Appendix H) — the delay and the faith question

- **9-12.AH.3.1** — Scientific knowledge is open to revision in light of new evidence: "Science explanations can change based on new evidence." *(The fixity of species, revised.)*
- **9-12.AH.7.1** — Scientific knowledge is a result of human endeavor, imagination, and creativity. *(Twenty years of notebooks, the barnacle dissection, the pigeon lofts.)*
- **9-12.AH.8.1** — Not all questions can be answered by science. *(His agnosticism, honestly held: "I must be content to remain one.")*
- **9-12.AH.8.2** — Science and technology may raise ethical issues for which science, by itself, does not provide answers and solutions. *(Knowing the book would wound Emma — and publishing.)*

### Practice

- **Science and Engineering Practice 7: Engaging in Argument from Evidence** (Appendix F) — "Evaluate the claims, evidence, and/or reasoning behind currently accepted explanations or solutions to determine the merits of arguments." *(The debrief's "was it honest to publish a theory with a hole at its center?")*

## 5. Other states (quick hits)

Grade band: **9–12** throughout.

### New York (NYSSLS science)

- **HS-LS4-1, HS-LS4-2, HS-LS4-4** (NYSSLS, adopted 2016, retains the NGSS performance-expectation codes).

### Texas (TEKS, 2021 science revision, §112.42 Biology)

- The student knows "evolutionary theory is a scientific explanation for the unity and diversity of life **that has multiple lines of evidence**" — including analyzing and evaluating how evidence of common ancestry among groups is provided by the fossil record, biogeography, and homologies, including anatomical, molecular, and developmental.
- The student knows "evolutionary theory is a scientific explanation for the unity and diversity of life **that has multiple mechanisms**" — including analyzing and evaluating how natural selection produces change in populations and not in individuals; how inherited variation, overproduction, and finite resources result in differential reproductive success; and how natural selection may lead to speciation.
- *(Text verified against the official SBOE "Proposed New 19 TAC" final-adoption document for the 2021 science TEKS revision (sboe.texas.gov); subsection numbering for the 2021 revision was not independently pinned and is not claimed here.)*

### Florida (State Academic Standards, 2023 adoption; CPALMS)

- **SC.912.L.15.1** — "Explain how the scientific theory of evolution is supported by the fossil record, comparative anatomy, comparative embryology, biogeography, molecular biology, and observed evolutionary change."
- **SC.912.L.15.13** — "Describe the conditions required for natural selection, including: overproduction of offspring, inherited variation, and the struggle to survive, which result in differential reproductive success."
- **SC.912.N.1.3** — "Recognize that the strength or usefulness of a scientific claim is evaluated through scientific argumentation, which depends on critical and logical thinking, and the active consideration of alternative scientific explanations to explain the data presented." *(The twenty-year delay as the gathering of alternatives.)*
- **SC.912.N.2.2** — "Identify which questions can be answered through science and which questions are outside the boundaries of scientific investigation, such as questions addressed by other ways of knowing, such as art, philosophy, and religion." *(The faith question, held exactly where the encounter holds it.)*

### California (CA NGSS)

- **HS-LS4-1, HS-LS4-2, HS-LS4-4** (California adopted the NGSS performance-expectation codes verbatim).

## Verification caveats

1. **C3 Framework**: indicator texts verified against three independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (in agreement); the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable this session. Official publisher: NCSS (2013), socialstudies.org/c3.
2. **No state history/social-studies anchor cited**: in all five states, natural selection is taught as science, not as history. The historical and civic work of the encounter is carried by the C3 Framework and the ELA standards above — which is the honest fit. (The Darwin teacher-instructions' condensed alignment already lists C3 D2.His.2.9-12 and D2.His.16.9-12 with correct indicator texts; no correction needed there.)
3. **Texas 2021 TEKS**: the SBOE document quoted is the final "Proposed New 19 TAC" text that became the adopted 2021 revision; the quotation marks around the two "student knows" statements are the document's own. Subsection numbers within §112.42 were not verified and are not claimed.
4. **Florida nature-of-science codes**: SC.912.N.1.3 and SC.912.N.2.2 texts verified via the Florida Senate's official 2008 bill analysis quoting the CPALMS benchmark text (NGSSS science benchmarks re-adopted under the 2023 State Academic Standards).
5. **Math**: no mathematics standard is cited — the encounter involves no quantitative work. (The four-factor structure of HS-LS4-2 is conceptual, not computational, in this encounter.)
