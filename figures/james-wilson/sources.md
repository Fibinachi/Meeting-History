## Authored devices

Dramatic devices the encounter uses that the history does not document. Plausible is not documented — say which.

- **The Edenton tavern-room setting** — early August 1798, a room above a tavern, Wilson on the circuit. Reported in secondary accounts (Penn Gazette; Mikhail law-review article: "holed up in a sweltering boarding room in Edenton"). Historically plausible and secondarily documented; not from Wilson's own hand.
- **Scripted opening and closing lines** — authored paraphrase built from his documented positions and period vocabulary; not verbatim quotation from any text.
- **The Convention speeches as given** — paraphrase from Madison's Notes; Madison reported, he did not transcribe. The voice's Convention answers are faithful in substance, not in wording.
- **"They say Washington himself attended the opening lecture"** — widely repeated in popular histories; secondary only. The prompt marks it with "they say" and lists it here.
- **The money answers' deflection** — the voice answers the debts with accounts and schemes, never conscience. This is the honest reading of his surviving late letters (practical, not confessional), raised to an encounter device. The silence is named in the prompt; it is not filled.

## Historical caveats

Things a teacher may be asked about, with the honest answer. Written before the figure ships, even the ones decided not to matter.

- **DEAD RULE: James Wilson died August 21, 1798, in Edenton, North Carolina — satisfied.** He was 55 (born September 14, 1742). One popular source (Penn Gazette) says "at age 56" — an error.
- **Convention words are Madison's reporting.** Treat Wilson's Convention lines as faithful substance, not transcript.
- **How many times was he jailed for debt?** Jailed in Philadelphia and again in Burlington, New Jersey (1797) — documented. Some accounts add a detention in North Carolina; the record is thin and it is marked uncertain in the prompt.
- **The slavery detail** (household slave freed 1794; spoke against slavery at the Convention but stayed quiet for ratification; proposed the three-fifths formula; objected to the Fugitive Slave Clause's wording) comes from modern secondary histories. Wilson left no systematic treatment of slavery in his own writings.
- **"The term sovereign is totally unknown"** — quoted from the original report's capitalization; the phrasing is Wilson's.
- **He knew the Eleventh Amendment.** Ratified 1795, overturning Chisholm — within his lifetime; the prompt lets him discuss it as fact.
- **He never saw the Lectures as a book.** Published 1804 by his son Bird Wilson; the voice correctly does not know it.
- **Remains reinterred 1906** at Christ Church, Philadelphia (from Johnston cemetery, Hayes Plantation, Edenton) — documented; belongs to the debrief, which he cannot know.
