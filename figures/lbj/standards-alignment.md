# Meet Lyndon B. Johnson — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: students interview LBJ at his Texas ranch (December 1972) about civil rights legislation, the Great Society, and the Vietnam escalation; they weigh his own words — including the secret White House tapes — against the record, and answer his closing question: *"I did more for civil rights than any president since Lincoln, and Vietnam undid me. Judge me."*

Key beats: questioning him until they understand his motives; noticing the tone shift when the storyteller becomes the arm-twister (the Treatment); holding his private admission to McGeorge Bundy ("the biggest damn mess") against his decision to escalate; evaluating the tapes — recorded without the other parties' knowledge — as evidence.

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12** (high school U.S. History).

---

## 1. C3 Framework (NCSS, 2013)

Strongest first. (Texts verified against curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim; the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable this session — see caveats.)

- **D2.His.11.9-12** — "Critique the usefulness of historical sources for a specific historical inquiry based on their maker, date, place of origin, intended audience, and purpose." The tapes are the perfect test case: maker = the president himself, audience = no one (recorded secretly), purpose = his own record-keeping.
- **D2.His.14.9-12** — Multiple and complex causes and effects of the civil rights movement's legislative victories and of the Vietnam escalation (the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution, the "crucified either way" bind).
- **D2.His.1.9-12** — Change and continuity in historical eras: the New Deal order continuing into the Great Society, then breaking under Vietnam.
- **D2.His.3.9-12** — How the significance of his actions changed over time: the split reputation (civil rights historians rank him among the greatest domestic presidents; the war destroyed his standing).
- **D2.His.4.9-12** — Complex, interacting factors shaping perspectives during the era — including his own stated reasons for staying in Vietnam.
- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrating evidence from multiple sources (tapes, speeches, memoir, the historical record) into a reasoned argument — the judgment itself.
- **D4.1.9-12** — Constructing the final judgment with evidence from multiple sources while acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses.

## 2. Common Core ELA — Literacy in History/Social Studies & Speaking/Listening

- **RH.11-12.6** — Evaluate differing points of view on the same event: his public "We Shall Overcome" address vs. his private, coarser voice on the tapes.
- **RH.11-12.3** — Evaluate competing explanations for his actions and determine which best accords with evidence: why did he escalate a war he called a mess?
- **RH.11-12.8** — Corroborate or challenge his claims with other information: his 1968 account of withdrawing "to work for peace" against the Tet/New Hampshire context (teacher notes).
- **RH.11-12.9** — Integrate tapes, speeches, memoir, and scholarship into a coherent understanding, noting discrepancies.
- **SL.11-12.3** — Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric: the Treatment *is* rhetoric to evaluate.
- **SL.11-12.1** — Collaborative discussion in the debrief; the judgment question produces structured disagreement.

*(Indicator texts verified against thecorestandards.org, 2026-10-15.)*

## 3. South Carolina (2019 Social Studies College- and Career-Ready Standards, US History and the Constitution)

- **USHC.5.CE** — "Assess the immediate and long-term causes and effects through significant turning points of the Cold War." (IXL's skill plan maps this to the Vietnam War — the escalation anchor.)
- **USHC.5.CC** — Evaluate continuities and changes during the Civil Rights Movement and other subsequent movements for equal rights. (The CRA 1964 / VRA 1965 anchor.)
- *Honest limit:* South Carolina's modern-era standards thin out after ~1970. USHC.5 ("Legacy of the Cold War") covers Vietnam but names no president; the indicator text does not mention Nixon or Watergate — those appear only in the IXL skill plan, not the standard.

## 4. Other states

**Texas (TEKS §113.41, U.S. History Since 1877).** Strongest fit in the set:
- **§113.41(b)(9)(F)** (adopted 2018 text) — "describe presidential actions and congressional votes to address minority rights in the United States, including desegregation of the armed forces, the Civil Rights acts of 1957 and 1964, and the Voting Rights Act of 1965." A direct LBJ anchor.
- **§113.41 (8)(F)** (2022 revision, implemented 2024–25) — covers the responses to the Vietnam War (draft, 26th Amendment, media role, credibility gap, silent majority, antiwar movement). The "credibility gap" language is the Vietnam-evaluation angle.

**Florida (State Academic Standards).**
- **SS.912.A.7.4** — "Evaluate the success of 1960s era presidents' foreign and domestic policies." Nearly a one-line description of the closing question.
- **SS.912.A.7.13** — "Analyze the attempts to extend New Deal legislation through the Great Society and the successes and failures of these programs to promote social and economic stability."
- **SS.912.A.7.10** — "Analyze the significance of Vietnam and Watergate on the government and people of the United States."

**California (HSS Content Standards, Grade 11).**
- **11.10.6** — Analyze the passage and effects of civil rights and voting rights legislation (1964 Civil Rights Act, Voting Rights Act of 1965), with emphasis on equality of access to the political process.
- **11.9.3** — Trace the origins and geopolitical consequences of the Cold War and containment policy, including the Vietnam War.
- **11.9.4** — Effects of foreign policy on domestic policies and vice versa (e.g., protests during the war in Vietnam).
- *Caveat:* no California content-standard code names the Great Society or LBJ; the closest fits are 11.10.6 (the legislation) and Florida's SS.912.A.7.13.

**New York (K–12 Social Studies Framework, Grade 11).**
- **11.10 / 11.10a** — SOCIAL AND ECONOMIC CHANGE/DOMESTIC ISSUES: the civil rights movement; performance indicator 11.10.A.III names the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965 explicitly.
- **11.9** — Key Idea: Cold War (1945–1990): U.S.–Soviet conflict "influenced foreign and domestic policy for more than 40 years."
- The NYSED framework resource also carries a performance indicator directing students to "examine reasons for declining public confidence in government, including America's involvement in Vietnam" — the Vietnam-accountability angle (key-idea letter not pinned in this session's retrieval; see caveats).
