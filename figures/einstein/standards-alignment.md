# Meet Einstein — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: students question Albert Einstein (April 1955) — the physics (relativity, the photoelectric effect), the 1939 letter to Roosevelt, his pacifism and its 1933 reversal, his stated regret — and at the end render one judgment: the pacifist whose letter helped build the bomb. Judge him.

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12** (high school physics / US History / ELA literacy).

---

## 1. C3 Framework (National Council for the Social Studies, 2013)

Grade band: **9–12**. (Indicator texts verified against independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim; the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable — see caveats.)

### Dimension 2 — History

- **D2.His.4.9-12** — Analyze complex and interacting factors that influenced the perspectives of people during different historical eras. *(The 1914 pacifist manifesto, the 1933 reversal under Hitler, the 1939 letter signed from fear of a German bomb — perspectives shaped by eras.)*
- **D2.His.5.9-12** — Analyze how historical contexts shaped and continue to shape people's perspectives.
- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrate evidence from multiple relevant historical sources and interpretations into a reasoned argument about the past. *(The judgment requires weighing his stated reason for signing against his stated regret.)*

### Dimension 4 — Communicating Conclusions and Taking Informed Action

- **D4.1.9-12** — Construct arguments using precise and knowledgeable claims, with evidence from multiple sources, while acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses.
- **D4.4.9-12** — Critique the use of claims and evidence in arguments for credibility. *(He insists he was never asked to build the bomb; the student must weigh that claim against the letter's role.)*

## 2. Common Core ELA — Literacy in History/Social Studies & Speaking/Listening

Grade band: **9–12**. (Verified against thecorestandards.org. The 9–10 band versions of these standards are exercised identically.)

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.1** — Cite specific textual evidence to support analysis of primary and secondary sources, connecting insights gained from specific details to an understanding of the text as a whole. *(The 1939 Einstein–Szilárd letter and the 1947 "would not have lifted a finger" interview are primary sources in the corpus.)*
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.6** — Evaluate authors' differing points of view on the same historical event or issue by assessing the authors' claims, reasoning, and evidence. *(His account of why he signed vs. scholarly accounts of the letter's role in the Manhattan Project.)*
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.9** — Integrate information from diverse sources, both primary and secondary, into a coherent understanding of an idea or event, noting discrepancies among sources.
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.3** — Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric, assessing the stance, premises, links among ideas, word choice, points of emphasis, and tone used. *(The debrief explicitly asks students to notice what he will and will not say — warmth on physics, quiet on family.)*
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.W.11-12.1** — Write arguments to support claims in an analysis of substantive topics or texts, using valid reasoning and relevant and sufficient evidence. *(The closing judgment, rendered in writing.)*

## 3. NGSS (Next Generation Science Standards, 2013)

Grade band: **9–12**. (Performance expectations verified via NSTA/nextgenscience.org reproductions; Appendix H understandings via the official Appendix H text.)

### Disciplinary Core Ideas

- **HS-PS1-8** — Develop models to illustrate the changes in the composition of the nucleus of the atom and the energy released during the processes of fission, fusion, and radioactive decay. *(The bomb's physics; mass-energy equivalence — his 1905 paper "Does the Inertia of a Body Depend Upon Its Energy Content?" — is the conceptual basis.)*
- **HS-PS4-3** — Evaluate the claims, evidence, and reasoning behind the idea that electromagnetic radiation can be described either by a wave model or a particle model, and that for some situations one model is more useful than the other. *(The photoelectric effect — his 1921 Nobel work — is named among the clarification statement's example phenomena.)*

### Nature of Science (Appendix H) — the letter's social dimensions

- **9-12.AH.7.5** — Science and engineering are influenced by society and society is influenced by science and engineering. *(A refugee's letter setting the Manhattan Project in motion.)*
- **9-12.AH.8.2** — Science and technology may raise ethical issues for which science, by itself, does not provide answers and solutions. *(The closing judgment is exactly such an issue.)*
- **9-12.AH.8.3** — Science knowledge indicates what can happen in natural systems — not what should happen.

### Practice & Crosscutting

- **Science and Engineering Practice 7: Engaging in Argument from Evidence** (Appendix F) — including "construct, use, and/or present an oral and written argument or counterargument based on data and evidence." *(The closing judgment.)*
- **Crosscutting Concept: Energy and Matter** — "In nuclear processes, atoms are not conserved, but the total number of protons plus neutrons is conserved."

## Verification caveats

1. **C3 Framework**: indicator texts verified against three independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (in agreement); the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable this session. Official publisher: NCSS (2013), socialstudies.org/c3.
2. **SC 2019 social studies**: the 2019 revision contains no explicit atomic-bomb indicator — the 2011 standards' USHC-7.3 ("the dropping of atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki") was removed in the 2019 rewrite. USHC.4.CO and USHC.4.E are the honest 2019 WWII-era anchors, verified against the official SCDE 2019 standards PDF.
3. **Texas Physics TEKS**: the official TEA TEKS Guide includes a special-relativity section (time dilation, length contraction, mass-energy equivalence, E=mc²) for high school physics; a standalone relativity student-expectation code in the 2021 Physics TEKS (§112.40) was not verified this session and none is claimed here.
4. **Math**: no mathematics standard is cited — the encounter involves no quantitative work.
5. **Einstein teacher-instructions note**: the condensed alignment on that page labels D2.His.4.9-12 with the indicator text of D2.His.5.9-12. The indicator texts above are the correct ones, verified per the C3 Framework; the teacher-instructions page should be corrected when the standards layer is condensed onto it.
