## Authored devices

- **The encounter itself.** No conversation between Einstein and a student in April 1955 is documented. The setting (his study at 112 Mercer Street, shortly after signing the Russell–Einstein Manifesto on 11 April 1955, one week before his death) is the library's dramatic device: historically plausible, not documented.
- **Condensed phrasing of documented statements.** The canonical answers compress documented remarks into the encounter's 60–100-word paragraphs. The sense and the facts are his; the exact sentence-by-sentence wording is the author's compression.
- **"The one great mistake in my life."** Widely reported as Einstein's remark to Linus Pauling (1954); provenance is secondhand (Pauling's recollection), not a published letter. The encounter states the regret as his own stated fact — keep the caveat here.

## Historical caveats

- **DEAD RULE satisfied.** Albert Einstein died 18 April 1955 at Princeton Hospital. He refused surgery for the ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm, reportedly saying he wanted to go when he wanted to go. The figure knows nothing after that date.
- **He did not write the Roosevelt letter.** Leo Szilárd drafted it (in English, from Einstein's dictated German); Einstein read it, chose the longer draft, and signed both versions on 2 August 1939. At the first meeting (12 July 1939) Einstein reportedly said *"Daran habe ich gar nicht gedacht"* — "I did not even think about that." He never claimed authorship; the encounter states this plainly.
- **He was not "the father of the atomic bomb."** He played no role in the Manhattan Project and was denied a security clearance in 1940, in part because of his pacifist record. The Time magazine cover (1 July 1946) with the mushroom cloud was a journalistic framing, not a fact about his role.
- **The Nobel Prize was not for relativity.** The 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded for the photoelectric effect paper — specifically for the light-quanta work — not for special or general relativity.
- **"God does not play dice" is a compressed, mild retheologization.** The real line (letter to Born, 4 December 1926): "I, at any rate, am convinced that He does not throw dice" — referring to the "Old One," Einstein's Spinoza's-God, the order of nature, not a personal deity. The canonical answer gives the real context; teachers should correct the slogan form if students arrive with it.
- **Bohr's retort ("Einstein, stop telling God what to do") is attested but its exact wording varies** (reported via Pais from Bohr's circle). The encounter does not put it in Einstein's mouth.
- **Lieserl.** Einstein and Mileva had a daughter, Lieserl (born 1902), before their marriage. Her fate is uncertain; the encounter marks the gap rather than filling it.
- **Eduard ("Tete").** His younger son developed schizophrenia and was institutionalized in Zürich; Einstein visited rarely. The family letters support the encounter's honesty on this; teachers should not let discussion drift into diagnosing anyone.
- **The 1933 pacifism reversal is real and documented** (letter to a Belgian conscientious objector, published in the New York Times, September 1933), not a later rationalization.
