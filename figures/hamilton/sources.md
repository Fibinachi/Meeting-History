## Authored devices

- **The setting (the evening of July 10, 1804).** A conversation on that night is a pure dramatic device — no such meeting is documented. It is the mechanism that lets Hamilton state his intent (documented in his pre-duel statement) without narrating the field.
- **The scripted opening and closing words.** The sentences are authored; every factual claim inside them is documented.
- **The stall lines and hostility path.** Authored in his register; the path's boundary (no modern apology, no narration of the field) follows the corpus.

## Historical caveats

- **Birth year uncertain.** Hamilton consistently gave 1757 after reaching North America, but earlier Caribbean documents suggest 1755. The prompt and debrief mark the uncertainty; the figure states neither year as fact.
- **Federalist attribution.** Hamilton's own posthumous list claimed more of the essays (66 of 85) than modern scholarship allows. The Adair attribution (1944, corroborated by statistical analysis in 1964) gives him 51 numbers; Nos. 18–20 are assigned to Madison, with some editions noting Madison–Hamilton collaboration. The prompt uses Adair's 51 and the debrief does not dwell on the dispute.
- **Who fired first at Weehawken is genuinely unresolved** — same as the aaron-burr figure holds. The only witnesses were the two seconds, who turned their backs to preserve deniability and later disagreed. This encounter adds Hamilton's own pre-duel statement of intent ("reserve and throw away my first fire"); whether he carried it out on the field, or whether his pistol discharged accidentally when he was hit, is disputed, as is the sincerity of the statement itself. The figure is forbidden from resolving it.
- **Did Hamilton's payments to James Reynolds involve public money?** Hamilton's account (the pamphlet) says the payments were private blackmail over the affair, not treasury speculation; the three congressmen who confronted him in 1792 accepted this. Callender charged otherwise; the corruption charge was never proven. The figure speaks from Hamilton's account; the debrief states it as his claim, not as settled fact.
- **The Reynolds Pamphlet dating.** Older printings of the Hamilton Papers date it August 31, 1797; Founders Online corrects this to August 25, 1797 (first publication). The encounter uses August 25.
- **The 1800 election.** The burr figure's materials record Hamilton's role in preferring Jefferson over Burr in the tied electoral college of 1800 and in blocking Burr from the New York governorship in 1804. This encounter does not make Hamilton narrate 1800's backroom dealing beyond what his corpus documents; the feud material is the cabinet war of 1792.
- **Jefferson's side of the feud** comes from Jefferson's corpus, not Hamilton's. The figure speaks Hamilton's case at full strength; the teacher instructions hold Jefferson's counter-record honestly (he saw Hamilton as the enemy of republican government and a monarchist at heart).
