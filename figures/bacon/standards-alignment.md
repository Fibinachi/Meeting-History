# Meet Francis Bacon — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: the student questions Francis Bacon — philosopher of the new method of honest thinking (the idols, induction, Salomon's House) and, as Lord Chancellor, a judge impeached in 1621 for taking bribes from suitors. His defense is stated with full conviction and never allowed to win by eloquence: the encounter closes on the judgment — *he said he took the gifts but they never swayed his judgment. Do you believe him? Judge him.*

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12** (high school world history / ELA literacy). Where a state places the era's content below grade 9, the grade-band mismatch is noted in that section.

---

## 1. C3 Framework (National Council for the Social Studies, 2013)

Grade band: **9–12**. (Indicator texts verified against curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim — Eastern Illinois University "C3 at a Glance" compilation, Houghton Mifflin Harcourt C3 correlation, and immigrationhistory.org standards page; the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable — see verification note.)

### Dimension 2 — Civics (principles vs. practice)

- **D2.Civ.10.9-12** — Analyze the impact and the appropriate roles of personal interests and perspectives on the application of civic virtues, democratic principles, constitutional rights, and human rights. (The encounter's engine: a judge's personal interest — gifts from suitors — set against the civic virtue of impartial judgment. His defense asks the student to accept that the interest never touched the virtue.)
- **D2.Civ.8.9-12** — Evaluate social and political systems in different contexts, times, and places, that promote civic virtues and enact democratic principles. (Jacobean England's Chancery, where gift-taking was, by Bacon's own account, "the custom of the office" — the question whether custom is a defense or the name corruption gives itself.)

### Dimension 2 — History

- **D2.His.3.9-12** — Use questions generated about individuals and groups to assess how the significance of their actions changes over time and is shaped by the historical context. (Rehabilitated for the method, never acquitted of the bribes — his reputation's two tracks.)
- **D2.His.10.9-12** — Detect possible limitations in various kinds of historical evidence and differing secondary interpretations. (Can the student check his claim that he "gave sentence against the very men who had paid me"? The third load-bearing nudge points exactly here.)
- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrate evidence from multiple relevant historical sources and interpretations into a reasoned argument about the past. (The closing judgment: integrate the Essays, the submission of 1621, and the impeachment record into one verdict.)

### Dimension 4 — Communicating Conclusions and Taking Informed Action

- **D4.1.9-12** — Construct arguments using precise and knowledgeable claims, with evidence from multiple sources, while acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses. (Believe him or not — the judgment must be argued, not felt.)
- **D4.4.9-12** — Critique the use of claims and evidence in arguments for credibility. (His defense is beautiful; the encounter's design is that beauty is not evidence.)

*D4 indicator texts verified 2026-10-15 against Greenwich Public Schools curriculum documents, the Kentucky Academic Standards social studies module, and the Teacher's Discovery C3 correlation (same verification pass as the Trial of John Brown alignment). Official document: College, Career, and Civic Life (C3) Framework for Social Studies State Standards, NCSS, 2013 (socialstudies.org/c3).*

## 2. Common Core ELA — Reading Informational Text, Writing, Speaking/Listening

Grade band: **9–12**. (Verified 2026-10-15 against Connecticut State Department of Education CCSS compilations (portal.ct.gov) and the California Educators Together standards entry.)

### Reading Informational Text, 11–12

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RI.11-12.6** — Determine an author's point of view or purpose in a text in which the rhetoric is particularly effective, analyzing how style and content contribute to the power, persuasiveness, or beauty of the text. (The strongest ELA fit in the library: Bacon's bribery defense is the case study — style and content separated, then weighed.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RI.11-12.1** — Cite strong and thorough textual evidence to support analysis of what the text says explicitly as well as inferences drawn from the text, including determining where the text leaves matters uncertain. ("More than once I gave sentence against the very men who had paid me" — what can the text establish, and where does it leave matters uncertain?)

### Writing, 11–12

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.W.11-12.1** — Write arguments to support claims in an analysis of substantive topics or texts, using valid reasoning and relevant and sufficient evidence. (The closing judgment rendered as an argued verdict.)

### Speaking & Listening

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.3** — Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric, assessing the stance, premises, links among ideas, word choice, points of emphasis, and tone used. (Pressing the eloquent defendant: which sentence was most persuasive, and what is it actually claiming?)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.1** — Initiate and participate effectively in a range of collaborative discussions with diverse partners, building on others' ideas and expressing their own clearly and persuasively. (The debrief discussion.)

*(SL codes verified against thecorestandards.org in the Trial of John Brown pass, 2026-10-15.)*

## 3. South Carolina — Social Studies College- and Career-Ready Standards (2019), Modern World History

Grade band: **9–12** (Modern World History is a high school course). (Verified 2026-10-15 against the official SC Department of Education 2019 standards document and the Houghton Mifflin Harcourt correlation.)

- **MWH.2.CX** — Contextualize the impact of the Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment in expanding global interactions in commerce and innovations. (Bacon's Novum Organum, 1620, and the method as an innovation with global reach.)
- **MWH.2.CO** — Compare and contrast the philosophies and practices of absolutism to the philosophies and practices of constitutional democracies. (Bacon rose and fell under James I's personal rule; his Chancery was the King's conscience.)
- **MWH.2.E** — Utilize a variety of primary and secondary sources to analyze multiple perspectives on global interactions. (The Essays, the 1621 submission, and the impeachment record read against each other.)

## 4. Other states (quick hits)

### Texas (TEKS, 2022 revision)

- **WH.1.E** — Identify major causes and describe major effects of important turning points in world history from 1750 to 1914: the Scientific Revolution, the Industrial Revolution, European imperialism, and the Enlightenment's impact on political revolutions. (See caveat 3: Texas groups the Scientific Revolution in the 1750–1914 band.)
- **WH.28.D** (social studies skills) — Evaluate a variety of historical and contemporary sources for validity, credibility, bias, and accuracy. (The defense, the plea, the sentence — three sources, one man's word.)
- **WH.28.F** — Formulate and communicate a claim supported by evidence and reasoning for an intended audience and purpose. (The verdict.)

*Verified against: Texas Social Studies Standards 2022 compilation (mkt.commonplanner.com). Note: Bacon is not named in the 2022 TEKS' scientist examples (WH.26.E lists Copernicus, Galileo, Newton, Boyle, et al.) — Texas's direct Bacon anchor is weaker than Florida's or California's; cited honestly.*

### New York (K–12 Social Studies Framework, Grade 9 — Global History and Geography I)

- **9.9d** — The development of the Scientific Revolution challenged traditional authorities and beliefs. (Students examine the Scientific Revolution, including the influence of Galileo and Newton; Bacon belongs to this inquiry as the method's formulator.)
- **9.9c** — Absolutist governments emerged as Western European and Russian monarchs consolidated power and wealth. (The Jacobean court that made and unmade him.)

*Verified against: NYS 9–12 Framework (revised Feb 2017) via framework PDF mirrors (nysacourse.weebly.com, 5il.co). Note: NY places this content in Grade 9, one band below the encounter's 9–12 target.*

### Florida (State Academic Standards, World History)

- **SS.912.W.4.10** — Identify the major contributions of individuals associated with the Scientific Revolution. - **SS.912.W.4.6** — Describe how scientific theories and methods of the Scientific Revolution challenged those of the early classical and medieval periods. (Induction against the syllogism — the encounter's middle movement.)
- **SS.912.W.4.5** — Describe how ideas from the Middle Ages and Renaissance led to the Scientific Revolution.

*Verified against: Florida course descriptions (flrules.org); commonplanner.com Florida World History compilation.*

### California (History–Social Science Content Standards, Grade 7)

- **7.10.3** — Understand the scientific method advanced by **Bacon** and Descartes, the influence of new scientific rationalism on the growth of democratic ideas, and the coexistence of science with traditional religious beliefs. (Bacon named directly; the standard's third clause — rationalism beside belief — is the encounter's own tension, method beside misconduct.)
- **7.10** — Students analyze the historical developments of the Scientific Revolution and its lasting effect on religious, political, and cultural institutions.
- **9-12.HR.3 / 9-12.HR.4** (Historical Research skills) — Evaluate debates among historians concerning alternative interpretations of the past; construct and test hypotheses from multiple primary and secondary sources. (The 9–12 skills anchor, since California places the content in Grade 7.)

## Verification caveats

1. **C3 Framework**: indicator texts verified against independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (they agree); the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable this session. Official publisher: NCSS (2013), socialstudies.org/c3.
2. **NGSS deliberately excluded**: the teacher instructions for this figure suggest NGSS Nature of Science (Appendix H). Per the library's scope rule (NGSS only for scientists — Bacon is a philosopher, not a scientist), it is not cited here. Noted so the omission is deliberate, not an oversight.
3. **Texas WH.1.E banding**: the 2022 Texas TEKS group the Scientific Revolution with the 1750–1914 turning points, though the revolution's core is 16th–17th century. Cited as written, with this noted. Texas's high-school US Government standards contain no bribery/impeachment-specific anchor that fits better than the C3 civics codes above.
4. **AP European History is a College Board course framework**, not an adopted state standard — cited separately and labeled as such.
5. **Grade-band mismatches**: New York (Grade 9) and California (Grade 7) place the Scientific Revolution below the encounter's 9–12 target; noted per section. The 9–12 anchor everywhere is the skills work (source evaluation, rhetoric analysis, argued judgment).
6. **C3 indicators are grade-band endpoints** ("by the end of Grade 12"), not grade-specific standards.
