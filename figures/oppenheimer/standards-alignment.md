# Meet Oppenheimer — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: students question J. Robert Oppenheimer (December 1966) — Los Alamos, Trinity, Hiroshima and Nagasaki, the hydrogen-bomb fight, and the 1954 security hearing — and at the end render one judgment: was he a good man?

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12** (high school US History / ELA literacy / physics).

---

## 1. C3 Framework (National Council for the Social Studies, 2013)

Grade band: **9–12**. (Indicator texts verified against independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim; the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable — see caveats.)

### Dimension 2 — History

- **D2.His.4.9-12** — Analyze complex and interacting factors that influenced the perspectives of people during different historical eras. *(The builder of the bomb who opposed the super; the patriot destroyed by the security state.)*
- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrate evidence from multiple relevant historical sources and interpretations into a reasoned argument about the past. *(The "was he a good man" judgment requires integrating the Los Alamos record, the GAC report, and the hearing transcript.)*

### Dimension 2 — Civics

- **D2.Civ.10.9-12** — Analyze the impact and the appropriate roles of personal interests and perspectives on the application of civic virtues, democratic principles, constitutional rights, and human rights. *(The 1954 proceedings: loyalty, due process, and the state's power over the individual — civic ideals vs. practice.)*
- **D2.Civ.14.9-12** — Analyze historical, contemporary, and emerging means of changing societies, promoting the common good, and protecting rights. *(His postwar push for international control of atomic energy.)*

### Dimension 4 — Communicating Conclusions and Taking Informed Action

- **D4.1.9-12** — Construct arguments using precise and knowledgeable claims, with evidence from multiple sources, while acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses.
- **D4.4.9-12** — Critique the use of claims and evidence in arguments for credibility. *(Strauss's majority opinion alleged "fundamental character defects"; the student must weigh the claim against the record.)*

## 2. Common Core ELA — Literacy in History/Social Studies & Speaking/Listening

Grade band: **9–12**. (Verified against thecorestandards.org. The 9–10 band versions are exercised identically.)

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.2** — Determine the central ideas or information of a primary or secondary source; provide an accurate summary that makes clear the relationships among the key details and ideas. *(The 1954 hearing transcript — thousands of pages of adversarial testimony — is the encounter's anchoring primary source.)*
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.6** — Evaluate authors' differing points of view on the same historical event or issue by assessing the authors' claims, reasoning, and evidence. *(Robb's questions vs. Oppenheimer's answers; the 2–1 Board split; the majority opinion vs. the dissent.)*
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.8** — Evaluate an author's premises, claims, and evidence by corroborating or challenging them with other information. *(The Chevalier "cock-and-bull story" admission under oath, tested against the transcript.)*
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.1** — Initiate and participate effectively in a range of collaborative discussions … building on others' ideas and expressing their own clearly and persuasively. *(The debrief runs as discussion of an unresolvable question.)*
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.3** — Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric, assessing the stance, premises, links among ideas, word choice, points of emphasis, and tone used. *(His literary circling and short-sentence landings; what he will not say.)*
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.W.11-12.1** — Write arguments to support claims in an analysis of substantive topics or texts, using valid reasoning and relevant and sufficient evidence. *(The closing judgment, rendered in writing.)*

## 3. NGSS (Next Generation Science Standards, 2013)

Grade band: **9–12**. (Performance expectations verified via NSTA/nextgenscience.org reproductions; Appendix H understandings via the official Appendix H text.)

### Disciplinary Core Ideas

- **HS-PS1-8** — Develop models to illustrate the changes in the composition of the nucleus of the atom and the energy released during the processes of fission, fusion, and radioactive decay. *(The weapon the laboratory built; the "super" he opposed was fusion.)*

### Nature of Science (Appendix H)

- **9-12.AH.7.5** — Science and engineering are influenced by society and society is influenced by science and engineering. *(Los Alamos as a wartime society remaking science; science remaking war.)*
- **9-12.AH.8.2** — Science and technology may raise ethical issues for which science, by itself, does not provide answers and solutions. *("The physicists have known sin" — a knowledge that cannot be lost.)*

### Practice

- **Science and Engineering Practice 7: Engaging in Argument from Evidence** (Appendix F) — "Evaluate the claims, evidence, and/or reasoning behind currently accepted explanations or solutions to determine the merits of arguments." *(The Gita-line gap is explicitly an exercise in weighing a claim against its evidence.)*

## 5. Other states (quick hits)

Grade band: **9–12** throughout.

### New York (K–12 Social Studies Framework, Grade 11; NYSSLS science)

- **11.8** (World War II) — Students examine "how technological advancements altered the nature of war and the extent of its devastation, including … President Truman's decision to use the atomic bomb against the Japanese cities of Hiroshima and Nagasaki."
- **11.8c** — In response to WWII and the Holocaust, students examine US efforts to prevent such human suffering. *(The international-control push.)*
- **11.9** (Cold War, 1945–1990) — The frame for the 1954 hearing, the Red Scare, and the security state.
- Science: **NYSSLS HS-PS1-8** (NYSSLS, adopted 2016, retains the NGSS performance-expectation codes).

### Texas (TEKS)

- **§113.41(c)(7)(D)** (US History Studies Since 1877) — "Analyze major issues of World War II, including the Holocaust; the internment of German, Italian, and Japanese Americans and Executive Order 9066; and the development of conventional and atomic weapons."

### Florida (State Academic Standards, 2023 adoption; CPALMS)

- **SS.912.A.6.6** — "Analyze the use of atomic weapons during World War II and the aftermath of the bombings."
- **SS.912.A.6.8** — "Analyze the effects of the Red Scare on domestic United States policy." *(The 1954 hearing's political context.)*
- **SS.912.A.6.10** — "Examine causes, course, and consequences of the early years of the Cold War (Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, NATO, Warsaw Pact)."
- **SS.912.A.6.11** — "Examine the controversy surrounding the proliferation of nuclear technology in the United States and the world." *(The October 1949 GAC recommendation against the crash program.)*
- **SS.912.A.1.2** — "Utilize a variety of primary and secondary sources to identify author, historical significance, audience, and authenticity to understand a historical period." *(The hearing transcript; text verified in the Trial of John Brown alignment, 2026-10-15.)*
- **SC.912.P.10.11** — "Explain and compare nuclear reactions (radioactive decay, fission and fusion), the energy changes associated with them and their associated safety issues."

### California (History–Social Science Content Standards; CA NGSS)

- **11.7.7** (Grade 11) — "Discuss the decision to drop atomic bombs and the consequences of the decision (Hiroshima and Nagasaki)."
- **11.7.5** (Grade 11) — "Discuss the constitutional issues and impact of events on the U.S. home front, including the internment of Japanese Americans." *(The domestic-security frame adjacent to the 1954 proceedings.)*
- Science: **CA NGSS HS-PS1-8** (California adopted the NGSS codes verbatim).

## Verification caveats

1. **C3 Framework**: indicator texts verified against three independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (in agreement); the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable this session. Official publisher: NCSS (2013), socialstudies.org/c3.
2. **SC 2019 social studies**: the 2019 revision contains no explicit atomic-bomb indicator — the 2011 standards' USHC-7.3 ("the dropping of atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki") was removed in the 2019 rewrite. The cited 2019 anchors are the honest WWII/Cold War indicators, verified against the official SCDE 2019 standards PDF.
3. **Texas**: the 1954-hearing/Red Scare material is carried by the Florida and New York anchors; no separately-coded Texas TEKS student expectation for the hearing itself was verified — §113.41(c)(7)(D) is the verified Texas anchor. A 2026 SBOE proposal draft for new social studies TEKS exists but is not adopted and is not cited.
4. **SS.912.A.1.2**: text carried from the Trial of John Brown alignment (verified 2026-10-15); the benchmark code is current under Florida's 2023 State Academic Standards adoption.
5. **Math**: no mathematics standard is cited — the encounter involves no quantitative work.
