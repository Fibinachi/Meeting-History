# Meet Plato — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: the student questions Plato (c. 428–c. 347 BC) on the Theory of Forms, the cave, the tripartite soul, and philosopher-kings — then weighs his certainty against the debrief's plain record (the dialogues are his art, not transcripts; Syracuse failed; the Seventh Letter's authorship is disputed; slavery unexamined). It closes with the judgment question: *is there a Justice itself — beyond what any city happens to call just?*

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12** (high school ELA literacy / Government / World History). Same caveat as Socrates: most states' *content* anchors for classical Greece sit in lower grades; the 9–12 weight here comes from argumentation, evidence, and civic-thought standards that are band-matched.

## 1. C3 Framework (National Council for the Social Studies, 2013)

Grade band: **9–12**. (Indicator texts verified against curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim; the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable in this session — see verification note at end.)

Note honestly: C3 has **no philosophy discipline** — its Dimension 2 disciplines are History, Geography, Economics, and Civics. The political-philosophy angle maps to D2.Civ indicators, not a "D2.Phil."

### Dimension 2 — Civics

- **D2.Civ.2.9-12** — Analyze the role of citizens in the U.S. political system, with attention to various theories of democracy, changes in Americans' participation over time, and alternative models from other countries, past and present. (Philosopher-kings are the archetypal "alternative model" — and the encounter stages a live confrontation between Plato's model and the democratic one, capped by the debrief's record that Athens' democracy voted to kill Socrates.)
- **D2.Civ.8.9-12** — Evaluate social and political systems in different contexts, times, and places, that promote civic virtues and enact democratic principles. (Students evaluate Plato's system against democratic principles — the encounter's anti-democratic argument forces this judgment explicitly.)

### Dimension 2 — History

- **D2.His.11.9-12** — Critique the usefulness of historical sources for a specific historical inquiry based on their maker, date, place of origin, intended audience, and purpose. (The Syracuse story is told with the Seventh Letter's disputed authorship named aloud — honest sourcing modeled inside the encounter; students weigh whether to trust the letter.)
- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrate evidence from multiple relevant historical sources and interpretations into a reasoned argument about the past. (The closing question is argued from the encounter's evidence: the forms, the failed Syracuse experiment, the dialogues-as-art admission.)

### Dimension 4 — Communicating Conclusions

- **D4.1.9-12** — Construct arguments using precise and knowledgeable claims, with evidence from multiple sources, while acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses.

*Verified against: crfcap.org Washington State Standards/C3 dashboard; cdn.fairfieldschools.org district C3 documents; c3teachers.org blueprint documents. Official document: NCSS, 2013 (socialstudies.org/c3).*

## 2. Common Core ELA — Literacy in History/Social Studies, Speaking/Listening & Writing

Grade band: **9–12**. (Verified against thecorestandards.org.)

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.6** — Evaluate authors' differing points of view on the same historical event or issue by assessing the authors' claims, reasoning, and evidence. (Plato's Syracuse account vs. the disputed letter; Plato's Socrates vs. the debrief's "no transcript exists." The student assesses claims, reasoning, and evidence on both sides.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.3** — Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric, assessing the stance, premises, links among ideas, word choice, points of emphasis, and tone used. (Plato's certainty is the evidence the student must evaluate — the teacher instructions frame it exactly this way.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.W.11-12.1** — Write arguments to support claims in an analysis of substantive topics or texts, using valid reasoning and relevant and sufficient evidence. (The closing question — Justice itself or only the city's word? — as post-encounter argument.)

*Verified against: https://thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RH/11-12/6/, https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/SL/11-12/3/, https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/W/11-12/1/.*

## 3. South Carolina — Social Studies College- and Career-Ready Standards (2019)

Grade band: **9–12** for USG; Grade 6 for the classical-civilizations anchor.

- **USG.1.ER** (United States Government, Foundations of Democracy) — Analyze the philosophical influences on core political principles in the American constitutional democracy. (The strongest SC fit in the library for Plato: his theory of justice, the forms, and the philosopher-king argument are among the classical philosophical influences on American constitutional thought — and the encounter's debrief interrogates exactly how much of that inheritance to accept.)
- **6.1.P** (Grade 6) — Analyze the shift from early to classical civilizations and the enduring contributions of classical civilizations. (State guidance names Greece among the four major classical civilizations; grade-band caveat below.)

*Verified against: ed.sc.gov official 2019 standards documents (Grade 6 standards). USG.1.ER text verified against documents quoting the 2019 SC standards (SC State House Tour standards doc; CCSD US Government curriculum guide).*

## Verification caveats

1. **C3 Framework**: indicator texts verified against multiple independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (consistent with each other); the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable this session. Official publisher: NCSS (2013), socialstudies.org/c3.
2. **No "D2.Phil."**: C3's Dimension 2 has no philosophy discipline; the political-philosophy angle is cited to D2.Civ indicators only.
3. **Grade-band split**: the 9–12 alignments for Plato are skills standards (RH, SL, W, D2.His/D4, USG). State *content* anchors for classical Greece are mostly below band (SC Grade 6, FL Grade 6, CA Grade 6); NY 9.3 is the on-band exception.
4. **Texas**: WH.1.B text verified against a standards compilation, not the TEA-hosted PDF — recheck before print use.
5. **"Alternative models" reading of D2.Civ.2.9-12**: the indicator's examples are American political systems; applying it to Plato's philosopher-kings as a historical "alternative model from other countries, past" is a legitimate but extended reading — disclosed here rather than hidden.
