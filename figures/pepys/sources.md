Note on the editions: Pepys wrote the diary in Shelton's tachygraphy shorthand and had it bound into six volumes, which he bequeathed to Magdalene College, Cambridge. John Smith deciphered the shorthand 1819–22. The 1825 Braybrooke edition and the 1893 Wheatley edition were expurgated — the sexual passages were suppressed. Latham & Matthews (1970–83) is the first complete edition and the scholarly standard; it prints everything, including the polyglot passages. The figure's voice is drawn from the diary's content, which is identical across editions except for the suppressed passages — and the suppressed passages are precisely the ones the encounter needs.

## Authored devices

Dramatic devices the encounter uses that the history does not document. Plausible is not documented — say which.

- **The meeting itself.** No record exists of Pepys receiving a visitor to be questioned about his life. Pure dramatic device — the library's standard conceit.
- **The setting (Clapham, late May 1703).** The facts are documented — Pepys lived at William Hewer's house in Clapham from 1690 and died there on 26 May 1703 — but the scene of him receiving a questioner is assembled, not recorded. Historically plausible, not documented.
- **"Tell me nothing."** When asked whether the diary is read after his death, Pepys half-suspects it might be and refuses the knowledge. The bequest of the bound volumes to Magdalene College is documented, so the *hope* is plausible; the refusal is a dramatic device to preserve date discipline. Not documented.
- **His pronunciation guide ("say it 'Peeps'").** Pepys's descendants pronounce the name "Peeps"; the opening uses it as a warmth device. Documented as family tradition, not from the diary.

## Historical caveats

Things a teacher may be asked about, with the honest answer. Written before the figure ships, even the ones decided not to matter.

- **DEAD RULE — satisfied.** Samuel Pepys died 26 May 1703 at Clapham and was buried at St. Olave's, Hart Street, beside Elizabeth. The encounter is set days before his death; he knows nothing after it.
- **Marriage date.** Most sources give 1 December 1655. One genealogical source notes a suggestion that an earlier ceremony may have been formalised on that date. The prompt says only "December 1655" and "when she was fifteen" — both safe.
- **Plague death toll.** "Near a hundred thousand" is the commonly cited figure for London; it is a modern estimate, not a number Pepys counted. The prompt has him mark this: "I will not swear to a number I did not count myself."
- **What he buried, and when.** Two separate burials, kept distinct in the prompt: during the plague (1665) his gold was sent to Brampton and buried in his father's garden by his father and Elizabeth; during the fire (3 Sept 1666) he buried his wine and Parmesan cheese in his own London garden. Students conflate these; the prompt does not.
- **The polyglot code.** Documented: Pepys recorded sexual encounters in a macaronic mixture of English, French, Spanish, Latin and Italian. The encounter states this fact and paraphrases; it never quotes the explicit passages. That restraint is deliberate and age-appropriate — see teacher instructions.
- **Deb Willet, 25 October 1668.** Documented in the diary: Elizabeth found Pepys embracing the maid. The prompt keeps to what the diary records (the discovery, her fury, his vows) and does not invent Elizabeth's inner life — see the "what he does not know" section.
- **"The fittest man in England."** From a documented October 1665 letter (Coventry to Pepys, via the Diary). Quoted accurately in the prompt.
- **Post-diary career.** Secretary to the Admiralty (1673), MP, President of the Royal Society (1684–86), Tower of London (May 1679 – March 1680), retirement to Clapham (1690) — all public record; he may state them if asked, but the encounter is built on the diary decade.
- **The fire's cause.** Begun in Thomas Farriner's bakery, Pudding Lane — as Pepys records it. Modern consensus: accidental. The prompt does not speculate beyond his account.
- **John Smith's decipherment (1819–22) and the 1825/1893/1970–83 publication history.** Documented; belongs to the debrief ("what came after"), never to the figure.
- **Elizabeth's feelings.** No account of hers exists. The figure speaks only of what he observed. The prompt marks this boundary explicitly.
