# Meet Samuel Pepys — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: the student questions Samuel Pepys — a 1660s London navy clerk looking back on the nine years he kept a daily diary (plague, Fire, Dutch wars, his marriage, his sins recorded in code). The diary is the entire corpus; the figure speaks only from what he wrote down himself. The encounter closes on the judgment: *ambitious, vain, hardworking, unfaithful — a whole man, in his own words. Judge him.*

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12** (high school world history / ELA literacy). Where a state places the era's content below grade 9, the grade-band mismatch is noted in that section.

---

## 1. C3 Framework (National Council for the Social Studies, 2013)

Grade band: **9–12**. (Indicator texts verified against curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim — Eastern Illinois University "C3 at a Glance" compilation and Teacher's Discovery C3 correlation; the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable — see verification note.)

### Dimension 2 — History

- **D2.His.3.9-12** — Use questions generated about individuals and groups to assess how the significance of their actions changes over time and is shaped by the historical context. (The diary sat unread in shorthand for a century; its significance was made entirely after his death.)
- **D2.His.5.9-12** — Analyze how historical contexts shaped and continue to shape people's perspectives. (A Restoration naval bureaucrat's view of plague, fire, and patronage.)
- **D2.His.6.9-12** — Analyze the ways in which the perspectives of those writing history shaped the history that they produced. (Pepys is his own fondest witness; the encounter's design is that his charm and his evidence are the same text.)
- **D2.His.8.9-12** — Analyze how current interpretations of the past are limited by the extent to which available historical sources represent perspectives of people at the time. (We have no account from Elizabeth; the figure refuses to speak for her inner life, and the prompt marks that boundary.)
- **D2.His.10.9-12** — Detect possible limitations in various kinds of historical evidence and differing secondary interpretations. (The polyglot code, the formulaic confessions followed by repetition — what the self-portrait hides and hurries past.)
- **D2.His.11.9-12** — Critique the usefulness of historical sources for a specific historical inquiry based on their maker, date, place of origin, intended audience, and purpose. (A private diary in shorthand, written for no reader — the purest possible case of this indicator.)
- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrate evidence from multiple relevant historical sources and interpretations into a reasoned argument about the past. (The closing judgment, built from nine years of diary evidence.)

### Dimension 4 — Communicating Conclusions and Taking Informed Action

- **D4.1.9-12** — Construct arguments using precise and knowledgeable claims, with evidence from multiple sources, while acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses. (The judgment must weigh the able administrator against the unwanted advances on servant girls, both from his own pen.)
- **D4.4.9-12** — Critique the use of claims and evidence in arguments for credibility. (Does "he wrote it down himself" strengthen or weaken the defense? The debrief's discussion openings are built for this.)

*D4 indicator texts verified 2026-10-15 against Greenwich Public Schools curriculum documents, the Kentucky Academic Standards social studies module, and the Teacher's Discovery C3 correlation (same verification pass as the Trial of John Brown alignment). Official document: College, Career, and Civic Life (C3) Framework for Social Studies State Standards, NCSS, 2013 (socialstudies.org/c3).*

## 2. Common Core ELA — Literacy in History/Social Studies & Speaking/Listening

Grade band: **9–12**. (RH/SL codes verified against thecorestandards.org in the Trial of John Brown pass, 2026-10-15.)

### Reading in History/Social Studies, 11–12

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.1** — Cite specific textual evidence to support analysis of primary and secondary sources, connecting insights gained from specific details to an understanding of the text as a whole. (The diary entries as primary source: the pigeons, the buried cheese, the red-cross doors.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.2** — Determine the central ideas or information of a primary or secondary source; provide an accurate summary that makes clear the relationships among the key details and ideas. (What the nine years add up to: ambition, administration, appetite.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.6** — Evaluate authors' differing points of view on the same historical event or issue by assessing the authors' claims, reasoning, and evidence. (Partial fit — see caveat 4: the "differing points of view" are Pepys-the-charming-narrator vs. the evidence Pepys-the-record convicts him with, inside one source.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.8** — Evaluate an author's premises, claims, and evidence by corroborating or challenging them with other information. ("I stayed at my post" checked against the flight of others; the plague death toll he refuses to swear to.)

### Speaking & Listening

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.1** — Initiate and participate effectively in a range of collaborative discussions with diverse partners, building on others' ideas and expressing their own clearly and persuasively. (The debrief discussion, which the teacher instructions frame as disagreement-producing.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.3** — Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric, assessing the stance, premises, links among ideas, word choice, points of emphasis, and tone used. (The encounter's core discipline: evaluating Pepys's charm as rhetoric — what he boasts of, what he hurries past.)

## Verification caveats

1. **C3 Framework**: indicator texts verified against independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (they agree); the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable this session. Official publisher: NCSS (2013), socialstudies.org/c3.
2. **Grade-band mismatches**: New York (Grade 9) and California (Grade 7) place the early-modern content below the encounter's 9–12 target band; noted per section. The content anchors are exact for those grades.
3. **The teacher instructions' condensed alignment** for this figure (D2.His.16.9-12, D4.1.9-12, RH.11-12.6, SL.11-12.3) was explicitly labeled "best-fit selections, pending the verification pass" — this document is that pass; all four codes verified and carried over, with the RH.11-12.6 partial-fit caveat below.
4. **RH.11-12.6 is a partial fit**: its letter concerns authors' differing points of view on the same event. Here the encounter has one author; the tension the student evaluates is between Pepys's charming self-presentation and the evidence the same text supplies against him. The debrief's discussion questions generate the differing interpretations. Cited with this limitation, not stretched.
5. **Florida skills anchor not verified**: no Florida world-history skills code for primary-source analysis was directly confirmed this session; the Florida section carries the content anchor (SS.912.W.4.8) only.
6. **C3 indicators are grade-band endpoints** ("by the end of Grade 12"), not grade-specific standards.
