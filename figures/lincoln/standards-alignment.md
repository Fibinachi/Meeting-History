# Meet Lincoln — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: students press a simulated Abraham Lincoln in March 1865 — prairie lawyer, sixteenth president, the man who destroyed American slavery while doubting the races could live together. They question him across emancipation's timing (the Greeley letter, the war measure), the August 1862 colonization address, the suspension of habeas corpus, and the visible growth from Charleston 1858 to the Hahn letter of 1864 to the Second Inaugural — holding both Lincolns at once, then rendering the closing judgment: the marble says saint; the record says a politician who grew. Judge the man.

**Grade band fit for the encounter: 9–12 (and undergraduate).** The Civil War era sits in Grade 8 in Texas and California (see caveats); Florida, South Carolina, and New York place it at 9–12. Indicator texts are paraphrased, not quoted, per this session's copyright handling; codes and grade bands were verified against the named sources on 2026-10-15.

## 1. C3 Framework (National Council for the Social Studies, 2013)

### Dimension 2 — History

- **D2.His.2.9-12** — Analyze change and continuity in historical eras. (The encounter's engine: Charleston 1858 against the Hahn letter of 1864 against the Second Inaugural of 1865 — what changed, what didn't, and the colonization position he never publicly retracted.)
- **D2.His.4.9-12** — Analyze complex and interacting factors that influenced the perspectives of people during different historical eras. (His stated constraints: constitutional power, the border states, the war as the forcing function; "events have controlled me.")
- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrate evidence from multiple relevant historical sources and interpretations into a reasoned argument about the past. (The corpus itself is the source set: speeches, letters, proclamations, the debates' record.)
- **D2.His.11.9-12** — Critique the usefulness of historical sources for a specific inquiry based on maker, date, origin, audience, and purpose. (The colonization address as performance for a Black delegation; the Greeley letter as wartime positioning; Douglass's 1876 oration as praise-as-limit.)

### Dimension 4 — Communicating Conclusions

- **D4.1.9-12** — Construct arguments using precise claims with evidence from multiple sources, acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses. (The verdict must hold the 1862 words and the 1865 words at once.)

*Verified against: curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (aedownload.net, womenshistory.org, manuals.plus). Official document: C3 Framework, NCSS, 2013 (socialstudies.org/c3); official NCSS PDF not directly retrieved this session.*

## 2. Common Core ELA

### Reading: Informational Text, 11–12

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RI.11-12.9** — Analyze 17th-, 18th-, and 19th-century foundational U.S. documents of historical and literary significance for themes, purposes, and rhetorical features. (The standard explicitly names Lincoln's Second Inaugural Address — the encounter's closing voice.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RI.11-12.6** — Determine an author's point of view or purpose in a text whose rhetoric is particularly effective, analyzing how style and content contribute to its power, persuasiveness, or beauty. ("Fondly do we hope… the judgments of the Lord are true and righteous altogether.")
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RI.11-12.8** — Delineate and evaluate reasoning in seminal U.S. texts, including works of public advocacy. (The Greeley letter's "paramount object"; the July 1861 message on habeas corpus.)

### Reading in History/Social Studies, 11–12

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.6** — Evaluate authors' differing points of view on the same issue by assessing claims, reasoning, and evidence. (Lincoln against Lincoln: 1858's equality-denial vs 1864's suggestion that Black soldiers vote.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.1** — Cite specific textual evidence connecting details to the whole. (The colonization address's "with their consent"; the Hahn letter's "the very intelligent.")

### Speaking & Listening

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.3** — Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric, assessing stance, premises, word choice, emphasis, and tone. (The examiner's discipline applied to a lawyer's cadence.)

## Verification caveats

1. **Grade-band split.** Texas and California place the Civil War in Grade 8 (§113.20 and HSS 8.10); the encounter targets 9–12. South Carolina (USHC.2), Florida (SS.912.A.2), and New York (11.3) all anchor it at the high-school band. The Texas 8.8(C) and California 8.10.4 anchors are nevertheless the most Lincoln-specific standards found in any state.
2. **Colonization has no standard.** No state standard names Lincoln's colonization advocacy; it is covered only inside the broad "causes and consequences of the Civil War" language. The encounter's de-mythologizing engine (the 1862 address, the unretracted position) is served by the primary-source-analysis standards (RH.11-12, RI.11-12, 8.10.4), not by a content code.
3. **AP note (not independently verified this session).** The teacher instructions cite AP U.S. History Period 5 (1844–1877) as the AP anchor; this was not checked against the College Board course framework in this verification pass.
