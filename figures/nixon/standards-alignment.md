# Meet Richard Nixon — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: students interview Nixon in San Clemente (summer 1978), after the memoir and the Frost interviews. He speaks in two registers — the strategist's orator and the tapes' unguarded voice — and the student must hold the memoir's case against the tapes' record. Closing question: *"I opened China and I disgraced the presidency. Judge me."*

Key beats: distinguishing the two registers; pressing the June 23, 1972 "smoking gun" answer and noticing what it leaves out; weighing his Frost admissions ("a reasonable man could call that a cover-up") against his line at "crime"; evaluating his claim that the tapes, not the cover-up, destroyed him; refusing to let understanding become acquittal.

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12** (high school U.S. History / AP U.S. History Period 8).

---

## 1. C3 Framework (NCSS, 2013)

Strongest first. (Texts verified against curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim; the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable this session — see caveats.)

- **D2.His.6.9-12** — "Analyze the ways in which the perspectives of those writing history shaped the history that they produced." *RN* (1978) is the encounter's central exhibit: a memoir written to rehabilitate, presented as the figure's defense, never as fact.
- **D2.His.11.9-12** — Critique the usefulness of historical sources by maker, date, place of origin, intended audience, and purpose. The tapes (recorded secretly by the president himself) and the memoir (written for vindication) are a matched pair for this exercise.
- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrate evidence from multiple sources and interpretations into a reasoned argument: memoir vs. tapes vs. Frost interviews vs. the debrief's plain record.
- **D2.Civ.8.9-12** — Evaluate social and political systems in different contexts that "promote civic virtues and enact democratic principles." Watergate is the stress test: the courts ordered the tapes produced (*U.S. v. Nixon*), Congress moved toward impeachment, the system held.
- **D2.His.3.9-12** — How the significance of his actions changed over time: the consistently low historians' rankings vs. his own "political execution" account.
- **D2.His.4.9-12** — Complex factors shaping perspectives during the era — including his conviction that the establishment took him down, presented as his belief, never as fact.
- **D4.1.9-12** — The closing judgment, built from multiple sources with counterclaims acknowledged.
- **D4.4.9-12** — Critique the use of claims and evidence in arguments for credibility: his "lapse of memory" statement about the June 23 tape is the practice case.

## 2. Common Core ELA — Literacy in History/Social Studies & Speaking/Listening

- **RH.11-12.6** — Evaluate the two Nixons' differing points of view by assessing their claims, reasoning, and evidence: the orator's "peace with honor" vs. the tapes' strategist.
- **RH.11-12.8** — Evaluate his premises, claims, and evidence by corroborating or challenging them: check the June-23 account against the transcript; check the "ended the war" claim against 1975.
- **RH.11-12.1** — Cite specific textual evidence (the June 23 transcript, the Frost admissions) to support analysis, connecting details to the whole.
- **RH.11-12.9** — Integrate information from diverse sources — memoir, tapes, interviews, public papers — noting discrepancies among them.
- **SL.11-12.3** — Evaluate the speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric: his Frost move — concede enough to sound honest, then draw the line at "crime."
- **SL.11-12.1** — Structured debrief discussion; the "same scale or different scale" question is built for it.

*(Indicator texts verified against thecorestandards.org, 2026-10-15.)*

## 3. South Carolina (2019 Social Studies College- and Career-Ready Standards, US History and the Constitution)

- **USHC.5.CE** — Assess the immediate and long-term causes and effects through significant turning points of the Cold War. (IXL's skill plan maps this strand to the Vietnam War and to "The presidency of Richard Nixon" — the détente/Vietnam anchor.)
- **USHC.5.P** — Summarize the changes in the major American political party platforms during the period. Genuinely Nixon-relevant: the Southern Strategy and the party realignment he engineered.
- **USHC.5.CX** — Contextualize domestic economic development and American national identity within global politics — the détente-as-identity angle (weaker fit; included with the caveat).
- *Honest limit:* as with LBJ, the indicator text names no president; Nixon appears only in the IXL skill plan, not the standard.

## 4. Other states

**Texas (TEKS §113.41, U.S. History Since 1877).**
- **§113.41(b)(10)(A)** (adopted 2018 text) — "describe Richard M. Nixon's leadership in the normalization of relations with China and the policy of détente." The China half of the closing question, named explicitly.
- *Watergate caveat:* no adopted TEKS names Watergate. The closest anchors are the social-studies-skills strand — the expectation to "evaluate a variety of historical and contemporary sources for validity, credibility, bias, and accuracy" (numbering changed in the 2022 revision implemented 2024–25; confirm against TEA) — and **(8)(F)** on responses to the Vietnam War, which includes the "credibility gap." Texas's constitutional-powers angle is the honest gap in this set.

**Florida (State Academic Standards).** Strongest state fit for the Watergate half:
- **SS.912.A.7.10** — "Analyze the significance of Vietnam and Watergate on the government and people of the United States." Names Watergate outright.
- **SS.912.A.7.11** — Analyze U.S. foreign policy as it relates to Asia and other regions (China opening, détente).
- **SS.912.A.1.2** — Utilize primary and secondary sources to identify author, historical significance, audience, and authenticity — the tapes as primary source.

**California (HSS Content Standards).**
- **11.9.3** — Trace the origins and geopolitical consequences of the Cold War and containment policy, including the Vietnam War.
- **11.9.4** — Effects of foreign policy on domestic policies and vice versa (e.g., protests during the war in Vietnam).
- *Caveat:* no California content-standard code names Watergate or the tapes. The abuse-of-power angle is best served in California through C3's D2.Civ.8.9-12 and the ELA corroboration standards, not a state content code.

**New York (K–12 Social Studies Framework, Grade 11).**
- **11.9** — Key Idea: Cold War (1945–1990): U.S.–Soviet conflict "influenced foreign and domestic policy for more than 40 years" (Vietnam, détente, China opening).
- The NYSED framework resource carries a performance indicator directing students to "examine reasons for declining public confidence in government, including America's involvement in Vietnam, student protests, the growing antiwar movement, and the Watergate affair" — the Watergate anchor (in the 11.9–11.10 sequence; sub-letter not pinned in this session's retrieval).
