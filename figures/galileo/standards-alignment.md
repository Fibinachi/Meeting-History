# Meet Galileo — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: students question Galileo through the tube, the *Dialogue*, the 1633 trial, and the abjuration, then render a judgment on his recantation: coward, pragmatist, or the only man in the room who understood what survival was for.

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12** (high school physical/earth science + ELA literacy; also adaptable to world history).

---

## 1. NGSS — Physical & Earth/Space Science (National Research Council Framework, adopted as NGSS 2013)

### Performance expectation — the content anchor

- **HS-ESS1-4** — Use mathematical or computational representations to predict the motion of orbiting objects in the solar system. (Clarification: emphasis is on Newtonian gravitational laws governing orbital motions, which apply to human-made satellites as well as planets and moons; DCI ESS1.B; practice SEP-5, Using Mathematics and Computational Thinking.) The encounter's observations — Jupiter's four moons circling a body other than Earth, Venus's phases proving it orbits the Sun — are the *empirical evidence base* behind this PE's model: orbital motion governed by gravity, not by Earth's centrality.
- *Caveat:* students in the encounter do not perform the PE's mathematical representation; the alignment is to the phenomenon and the historical evidence for it, not to the computation itself.

### Disciplinary core idea

- **ESS1.B: Earth and the Solar System** — Kepler's laws describe common features of the motions of orbiting objects. Galileo's telescopic observations are the observations that made the heliocentric account unavoidable.

### Nature of science (NGSS Appendix H, "Understanding the Scientific Enterprise")

These are the strongest NGSS fits for this figure — the trial is a nature-of-science case study:

- **AH.1** — Scientific investigations use a variety of methods. (The tube is a new instrument producing a new kind of evidence.)
- **AH.2** — Scientific knowledge is based on empirical evidence. (Mountains on the Moon, the Medicean stars, Venus's phases — evidence against Aristotelian perfection.)
- **AH.3** — Scientific knowledge is open to revision in light of new evidence. (The encounter shows a worldview being revised *and* resisted.)
- **AH.7** — Science is a human endeavor. (The trial: authority, patronage, and institutional power acting on the knowledge claim.)
- **AH.8** — Science addresses questions about the natural and material world. (The Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina is the period's own articulation of this boundary — "how one goes to heaven, not how heaven goes.")

*Verified against: NSTA NGSS display (my.nsta.org), Smithsonian Learning Lab NGSS topic page, CA Dept of Education content-standards browser (HS-ESS1-4, CA NGSS), and NGSS Appendix H via the official-text mirror (commonplanner). Official documents: NGSS Lead States (2013), nextgenscience.org.*

## 2. C3 Framework (National Council for the Social Studies, 2013)

### Dimension 2 — History

- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrate evidence from multiple relevant historical sources and interpretations into a reasoned argument about the past. (The closing judgment: trial record + debrief record + legend, weighed together.)
- **D2.His.4.9-12** — Analyze complex and interacting factors that influenced the perspectives of people during different historical eras. (Counter-Reformation Rome, patronage, the Decree of 1616.)
- **D2.His.8.9-12** — Analyze how current interpretations of the past are limited by the extent to which available historical sources represent perspectives of people at the time. (The legend of "Eppur si muove," first printed 1757, vs. the trial record.)
- **D2.His.11.9-12** — Critique the usefulness of historical sources for a specific historical inquiry based on their maker, date, place of origin, intended audience, and purpose. (The trial record is adversarial — testimony given under examination by a tribunal building a conviction.)

### Dimension 3 — Evaluating Sources and Using Evidence

- **D3.1.9-12** — Gather relevant information from multiple sources representing a wide range of views while using the origin, authority, structure, context, and corroborative value of the sources to guide the selection. (Bellarmine's certificate vs. the unsigned 1616 memorandum.)

### Dimension 4 — Communicating Conclusions and Taking Informed Action

- **D4.1.9-12** — Construct arguments using precise and knowledgeable claims, with evidence from multiple sources, while acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses. (Coward / pragmatist / survivor — each claim must be evidenced.)
- **D4.4.9-12** — Critique the use of claims and evidence in arguments for credibility. (Killing the legend on sight.)

*Verified against: independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (Michigan History Day guide, Minnesota Historical Society, Greenwich Public Schools, National History Day, Truman Library). Official NCSS PDF not directly retrievable this session — treat NCSS (2013), socialstudies.org/c3, as publisher.*

## 3. Common Core ELA — Literacy in History/Social Studies & Science, Speaking/Listening, Writing

### Reading in History/Social Studies, 11–12

The trial record and the *Dialogue* are historical primary sources; the legend-vs-record work is RH work:

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.1** — Cite specific textual evidence to support analysis of primary and secondary sources, connecting insights gained from specific details to an understanding of the text as a whole.
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.2** — Determine the central ideas or information of a primary or secondary source; provide an accurate summary that makes clear the relationships among the key details and ideas.
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.6** — Evaluate authors' differing points of view on the same historical event or issue by assessing the authors' claims, reasoning, and evidence. (Galileo's account vs. the sentence; Baretti's 1757 legend vs. the record.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.9** — Integrate information from diverse sources, both primary and secondary, into a coherent understanding of an idea or event, noting discrepancies among sources.

### Reading in Science & Technical Subjects, 11–12

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RST.11-12.7** — Integrate and evaluate multiple sources of information presented in diverse formats and media (e.g., quantitative data, video, multimedia) in order to address a question or solve a problem. (Telescopic observations + the *Dialogue* + trial record, integrated toward the judgment.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RST.11-12.8** — Evaluate the hypotheses, data, analysis, and conclusions in a science or technical text, verifying the data when possible and corroborating or challenging conclusions with other sources of information. (What the tube showed vs. what was claimed about it.)

### Speaking & Listening

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.1** — Initiate and participate effectively in a range of collaborative discussions with diverse partners, building on others' ideas and expressing their own clearly and persuasively. (The debrief's structured disagreement.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.3** — Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric, assessing the stance, premises, links among ideas, word choice, points of emphasis, and tone used. (The two registers: brilliant about the heavens, careful about Rome.)

### Writing in History/Social Studies, Science, and Technical Subjects

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.WHST.11-12.1** — Write arguments focused on discipline-specific content. (The written form of the closing judgment: a precise claim, counterclaims acknowledged, evidence from the record.)

*Verified against: thecorestandards.org (RH/SL, cross-confirmed via CCSS-reproducing district documents); NJ Student Learning Standards ELA Companion 11–12 (nj.gov — RST.11-12.7, RST.11-12.8, WHST.11-12.1 verbatim); California Educators Together (RST.11-12.7).*

## Verification caveats

1. **C3 Framework**: indicator texts verified against five independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (all agree); the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable this session. Publisher: NCSS (2013).
2. **NGSS mathematics caveat**: HS-ESS1-4/E-ESS1-4 call for mathematical or computational representations; the encounter is qualitative and historical. The alignment is to the phenomenon, the DCI, and the nature-of-science dimensions — do not present it as covering the PE's mathematical practice.
3. **Appendix H**: verified against the official Appendix H text (2013) via a standards mirror; the 9–12 elaborations carry the same AH.1–AH.8 category structure as the lower bands quoted in verification.
4. **No math standards cited**: the encounter involves no mathematical work, so no Common Core Math practice is claimed — deliberately.
