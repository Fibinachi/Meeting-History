# Meet Socrates — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: the form is INVERTED — Socrates questions the student (the elenchus). The student names things they claim to know, formulates positions under questioning, watches their own answers collapse under calm questions, and decides whether their claims hold. It closes with the handoff question: *is the unexamined life worth living?*

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12** (high school ELA literacy / Government / World History). Note: most states' *content* anchors for classical Greece sit in lower grades (see caveats); the 9–12 weight of this encounter comes from skills standards — discussion, argumentation, deliberation — which are band-matched.

## 1. C3 Framework (National Council for the Social Studies, 2013)

Grade band: **9–12**. (Indicator texts verified against curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim; the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable in this session — see verification note at end.)

This encounter is the rare one where the strongest C3 fits are **civics and Dimension 4**, not history. Note honestly: C3 has **no philosophy discipline** — its Dimension 2 disciplines are History, Geography, Economics, and Civics. The philosophy/civic-thought angle maps to D2.Civ indicators, not a "D2.Phil."

### Dimension 2 — Civics

- **D2.Civ.2.9-12** — Analyze the role of citizens in the U.S. political system, with attention to various theories of democracy, changes in Americans' participation over time, and alternative models from other countries, past and present. (Athenian direct democracy — the city whose jury voted to kill him — is the "alternative model from other countries, past" against which the debrief examines rule by the many.)
- **D2.Civ.9.9-12** — Use appropriate deliberative processes in multiple settings. (The elenchus *is* the deliberative process the student practices: stating a position, testing it, abandoning or revising it.)

### Dimension 4 — Communicating Conclusions and Taking Informed Action

- **D4.1.9-12** — Construct arguments using precise and knowledgeable claims, with evidence from multiple sources, while acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses. (The student builds claims in real time and is forced to acknowledge where their own claims break.)
- **D4.4.9-12** — Critique the use of claims and evidence in arguments for credibility. (The whole encounter trains this — first on the student's own claims, then on Socrates' single certainty that doing wrong is worse than suffering it.)

*Verified against: crfcap.org Washington State Standards/C3 dashboard; cdn.fairfieldschools.org district C3 documents; c3teachers.org blueprint documents. Official document: College, Career, and Civic Life (C3) Framework for Social Studies State Standards, NCSS, 2013 (socialstudies.org/c3).*

## 2. Common Core ELA — Speaking/Listening & Writing

Grade band: **9–12**. (Verified against the official Common Core State Standards Initiative site, thecorestandards.org.)

### Speaking & Listening, 11–12

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.1** — Initiate and participate effectively in a range of collaborative discussions (one-on-one, in groups, and teacher-led) with diverse partners on grades 11–12 topics, texts, and issues, building on others' ideas and expressing their own clearly and persuasively. (The encounter *is* a sustained one-on-one collaborative discussion; the student must express positions clearly while being pressed.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.3** — Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric, assessing the stance, premises, links among ideas, word choice, points of emphasis, and tone used. (Inverted use: the student watches Socrates dissect claims by premises and word choice — "what do you mean by that?" — and learns to apply the same evaluation to their own answers.)

### Writing, 11–12

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.W.11-12.1** — Write arguments to support claims in an analysis of substantive topics or texts, using valid reasoning and relevant and sufficient evidence. (The closing question — is the unexamined life worth living? — is designed as a post-encounter argument prompt, per the teacher instructions.)

*Verified against: https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/SL/11-12/1/, https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/SL/11-12/3/, https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/W/11-12/1/.*

## 3. South Carolina — Social Studies College- and Career-Ready Standards (2019)

Grade band: **9–12** for USG; Grade 6 for the classical-civilizations anchor.

- **USG.1.ER** (United States Government, Foundations of Democracy) — Analyze the philosophical influences on core political principles in the American constitutional democracy. (The encounter examines Socrates' ideas — the examined life, obedience to law in the *Crito*, the relation of the soul to the city — that are among those philosophical influences.)
- **6.1.P** (Grade 6) — Analyze the shift from early to classical civilizations and the enduring contributions of classical civilizations. (State guidance names Greece among the four major classical civilizations whose cultural and technological advancements "continue to influence modern societies." Grade-band caveat: this is the only SC content anchor naming classical Greece, and it sits in Grade 6.)

*Verified against: ed.sc.gov official 2019 standards documents (Grade 6 standards; USHC printable). USG.1.ER text verified against documents quoting the 2019 SC standards (SC State House Tour standards doc; CCSD US Government curriculum guide).*

## Verification caveats

1. **C3 Framework**: indicator texts were verified against multiple independent curriculum documents that quote the Framework verbatim (they agree with each other); the official NCSS PDF itself was not directly retrievable this session. Treat the official publisher as NCSS (2013), socialstudies.org/c3.
2. **No "D2.Phil."**: C3's Dimension 2 has no philosophy discipline. The civic-thought angle of this encounter is cited to D2.Civ indicators only; do not present a philosophy indicator as if it exists.
3. **Grade-band split**: the 9–12 alignments for Socrates are skills standards (SL, W, D4, USG). Every state except SC's US Government standard places classical-Greece content below the 9–12 band (SC Grade 6, NY Grades 9–10 — the one on-band exception, FL Grade 6/9–12 split, CA Grade 6).
4. **Texas**: WH.1.B indicator text verified against a standards compilation, not the TEA-hosted PDF. Recheck before citing in print.
5. **No RH fit claimed for the encounter proper**: the encounter is conversational, not text-based; reading standards (RH) engage mainly in the debrief (e.g., *Apology* 38a/21d/29b-c) and are not cited above.
