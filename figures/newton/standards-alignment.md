# Meet Isaac Newton — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: students question Newton on gravity, the prism experiments, and the *Principia* — then press into the Leibniz priority feud, the staged Royal Society verdict, the Mint, and the alchemy manuscripts. Closing judgment: the genius who remade the world and ruined a rival to do it. Judge him.

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12** (high school physics + ELA literacy).

---

## 1. NGSS — Physical Science (NGSS 2013)

### Performance expectations — the content anchors

- **HS-PS2-4** — Use mathematical representations of Newton's Law of Gravitation and Coulomb's Law to describe and predict the gravitational and electrostatic forces between objects. (DCI PS2.B: Types of Interactions.) This is the single strongest NGSS anchor for the encounter — the inverse-square law Newton expounds in his own words.
- **HS-PS2-1** — Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law of motion describes the mathematical relationship among the net force on a macroscopic object, its mass, and its acceleration. (Clarification statement's examples include "a falling object" — the falling-bodies work of the *Discorsi* that Newton's century inherits; the teacher instructions note Galileo's falling bodies as Newton's ground.)
- **HS-PS4-3** — Evaluate the claims, evidence, and reasoning behind the idea that electromagnetic radiation can be described either by a wave model or a particle model, and that for some situations one model is more useful than the other. (Clarification: emphasis is on how the experimental evidence supports the claim and how a theory is generally modified in light of new evidence.) The encounter's optics thread — Newton's prism experiments and his corpuscular theory of light, later contested by wave accounts — is the historical instance of the models debate this PE asks students to evaluate.
- *Caveats:* HS-PS2-4's mathematical-representation practice is not performed in the encounter (the work is qualitative and historical). HS-PS4-3 names experimental evidence, not historical biography — the alignment is to the evidence-evaluation framing, with Newton's prism work as the case.

### Disciplinary core ideas

- **PS2.A: Forces and Motion** — Newton's second law accurately predicts changes in the motion of macroscopic objects. (HS-PS2-1's DCI.)
- **PS2.B: Types of Interactions** — Newton's law of gravitation and Coulomb's law describe interactions; also the DCI behind MS-level "gravitational interactions are attractive," carried forward at high school in HS-PS2-4.

### Nature of science (NGSS Appendix H)

The Leibniz feud is a nature-of-science case study on credit, evidence, and misconduct:

- **AH.7** — Science is a human endeavor. (Priority, rivalry, institutional power: Newton appointing the committee that judged his own case, then anonymously reviewing its verdict.)
- **AH.2** — Scientific knowledge is based on empirical evidence. (The load-bearing nuance the encounter insists on: the *Commercium Epistolicum* letters were genuine, even though the verdict was staged — method and evidence judged separately.)
- **AH.3** — Scientific knowledge is open to revision in light of new evidence. (Modern consensus: independent invention; Leibniz's notation — the *dx* and ∫ — is what the world kept.)
- **AH.1** — Scientific investigations use a variety of methods. (The alchemy manuscripts: decades of laboratory work by methods the period did not call science, and the "science is not a straight line" angle.)

## 2. C3 Framework (National Council for the Social Studies, 2013)

### Dimension 2 — History

- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrate evidence from multiple relevant historical sources and interpretations into a reasoned argument about the past. (The closing judgment: Newton's own account weighed against the documentary record of the staged verdict.)
- **D2.His.4.9-12** — Analyze complex and interacting factors that influenced the perspectives of people during different historical eras. (The early-modern world of patronage, priority, and the Royal Society.)
- **D2.His.11.9-12** — Critique the usefulness of historical sources for a specific historical inquiry based on their maker, date, place of origin, intended audience, and purpose. (The *Commercium Epistolicum* and Newton's anonymous "Account" of it — sources whose maker and purpose are the inquiry.)

### Dimension 4 — Communicating Conclusions and Taking Informed Action

- **D4.1.9-12** — Construct arguments using precise and knowledgeable claims, with evidence from multiple sources, while acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses. (The judgment must hold the genius and the ruthlessness together.)
- **D4.4.9-12** — Critique the use of claims and evidence in arguments for credibility. (The apple legend vs. Stukeley's memoir; the Hooke portrait story; the genuine letters inside a rigged proceeding.)

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RST.11-12.8** — Evaluate the hypotheses, data, analysis, and conclusions in a science or technical text, verifying the data when possible and corroborating or challenging conclusions with other sources of information. - **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RST.11-12.6** — Analyze the author's purpose in providing an explanation, describing a procedure, or discussing an experiment in a text, identifying important issues that remain unresolved. (Newton's anonymous "Account" of the verdict in the *Philosophical Transactions* — authorship and purpose are the lesson.)

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.6** — Evaluate authors' differing points of view on the same historical event or issue by assessing the authors' claims, reasoning, and evidence. (Newton's account of the priority dispute vs. the documentary record — the condensed alignment's anchor, retained.)

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.1** — Initiate and participate effectively in a range of collaborative discussions, building on others' ideas and expressing their own clearly and persuasively. (The debrief's structured disagreement.)

## Verification caveats

1. **C3 Framework**: indicator texts verified against five independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (all agree); the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable this session. Publisher: NCSS (2013).
2. **NGSS practice caveat**: HS-PS2-1/HS-PS2-4/HS-PS4-3 call for data analysis, mathematical representation, and evidence evaluation that the encounter addresses historically and qualitatively rather than computationally. Do not present the encounter as delivering the PE's practice dimension.
3. **Optics honesty**: NGSS treats light through the wave/particle-model debate (HS-PS4-3), not through dispersion; Newton's prism work is cited as the historical case for the models-evaluation framing, not as direct coverage of a dispersion standard (none exists at HS level).
4. **No Common Core Math cited**: the encounter involves no mathematical work.
