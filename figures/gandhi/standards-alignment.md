# Meet Gandhi — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: the student interviews Mohandas K. Gandhi at Birla House, Delhi, in January 1948 — his fast for communal peace just ended, partition's blood still fresh — pressing him on satyagraha's power and its limits, and on the early South African writings he refuses to soften or explain away. It closes with the judgment question: *he freed a nation without firing a shot — and his early pages carry the racism of his time. Judge him whole.*

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12** (high school World History / ELA literacy). Note: the strongest SC content anchor (MWH.5.CE) covers 1945–1975 while the encounter reaches back to 1909; the encounter's pre-1945 material is covered by the perspective/argumentation indicators and by NY 10.7a, which names him.

---

## 1. C3 Framework (National Council for the Social Studies, 2013)

Grade band: **9–12**. (Indicator texts verified against curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim; the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable in this session — see verification note at end.)

### Dimension 2 — History

- **D2.His.4.9-12** — Analyze complex and interacting factors that influenced the perspectives of people during different historical eras. (The young Gandhi of the *Indian Opinion* years (1903–1908) writes from inside a white-supremacist colony as a young lawyer fighting for Indians' dignity; the 1942 letter to Roosevelt names Africa's exploitation alongside India's — the encounter demands the student hold both eras' perspectives in one man.)
- **D2.His.5.9-12** — Analyze how historical contexts shaped and continue to shape people's perspectives. (His early pages — "Indians must not be classed with the native Africans," the call for whites to remain "the predominating race" — are presented in the encounter as products of the colonial context he lived in, stated plainly rather than explained away; the student judges whether context explains or merely describes.)
- **D2.His.16.9-12** — Integrate evidence from multiple relevant historical sources and interpretations into a reasoned argument about the past. (The closing judgment requires integrating *Hind Swaraj*, the *Autobiography*, *Indian Opinion*, and *Young India*/*Harijan* — the salt at Dandi *and* the early South African pages — into one verdict.)

### Dimension 4 — Communicating Conclusions and Taking Informed Action

- **D4.1.9-12** — Construct arguments using precise and knowledgeable claims, with evidence from multiple sources, while acknowledging counterclaims and evidentiary weaknesses. (The closing question — judge him whole — is a designed argument prompt: the power of satyagraha (salt, fasts, making empire unworkable) against its admitted limits (it could not stop partition's blood), with the evidentiary weakness named honestly — the 1908 "commingle" line is secondary-sourced, per sources.md.)
- **D4.4.9-12** — Critique the use of claims and evidence in arguments for credibility. (The encounter's candor instruction — "do not soften, do not explain away, do not invent a retraction" — trains the student to critique exactly the kind of claim the record cannot support: a retraction that never happened.)

*Verified against: multiple independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (agreement among them). Official document: College, Career, and Civic Life (C3) Framework for Social Studies State Standards, NCSS, 2013 (socialstudies.org/c3).*

## 2. Common Core ELA — Literacy in History/Social Studies & Speaking/Listening

Grade band: **9–12**. (Verified against the official Common Core State Standards Initiative site, thecorestandards.org.)

### Reading in History/Social Studies, 9–10

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.9-10.1** — Cite specific textual evidence to support analysis of primary and secondary sources, attending to such features as the date and origin of the information. (*Hind Swaraj* written aboard the *Kildonan Castle* 13–22 November 1909, banned as seditious 1910; the *Autobiography* serialized 1925–1929 — date and origin are load-bearing for judging both texts.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.9-10.6** — Compare the point of view of two or more authors for how they treat the same or similar topics, including which details they include and emphasize in their respective accounts. (The encounter's central evidence move: the 1906 Zulu rising as told in the early *Indian Opinion* writing versus the *Autobiography*'s reframing as "no war but a man hunt" — what changed, what did not.)

### Reading in History/Social Studies, 11–12

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.6** — Evaluate authors' differing points of view on the same historical event or issue by assessing the authors' claims, reasoning, and evidence. (Gandhi's own account of his method — "truth-force, never passivity" — against the debrief's later judgments: Martin Luther King drawing on satyagraha, Mandela's verdict on the young Gandhi's prejudices, the 2018 Ghana statue removal.)
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.RH.11-12.8** — Evaluate an author's premises, claims, and evidence by corroborating or challenging them with other information. (Testing his "what satyagraha could not do" answer — the claim that satyagraha moves the heart of an opponent but not a mob — against the documented fasts at Calcutta and Delhi and the blood that flowed anyway.)

### Speaking & Listening, 11–12

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.1** — Initiate and participate effectively in a range of collaborative discussions (one-on-one, in groups, and teacher-led) with diverse partners on grades 11–12 topics, texts, and issues, building on others' ideas and expressing their own clearly and persuasively. (The encounter *is* a sustained one-on-one discussion with the examiner's posture built in: "press him the way an examiner presses a witness.")
- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.11-12.3** — Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric, assessing the stance, premises, links among ideas, word choice, points of emphasis, and tone used. (His signature rhetoric is the evidence: the experiment framing, the refusal to use modern moral vocabulary about himself, the stillness in the early-writings answers — the student evaluates what the gentleness is doing.)

### Writing, 11–12

- **CCSS.ELA-Literacy.W.11-12.1** — Write arguments to support claims in an analysis of substantive topics or texts, using valid reasoning and relevant and sufficient evidence. (The closing question — judge him whole — is designed as a post-encounter argument prompt, per the teacher instructions.)

*Verified against: https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RH/9-10/, https://thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RH/11-12/, https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/SL/11-12/1/.*

## 3. South Carolina — Social Studies College- and Career-Ready Standards (2019), Modern World History

Grade band: **9–12** (high school Modern World History). (Verified against the official SC Department of Education 2019 standards document, ed.sc.gov.)

- **MWH.5.CE** (Modern Age 1933–present) — Analyze significant developments resulting from post-war decolonization in Asia and Africa in the creation of the new countries during the period 1945–1975. (The Salt March, the Quit India years, and 1947 independence and partition are the encounter's decolonization content. Boundary note: the indicator's 1945–1975 window covers independence and partition but not the 1909–1945 material — the earlier campaigns are reached through the C3/RH indicators above.)
- **MWH.5.E** (Modern Age 1933–present) — Utilize a variety of primary and secondary sources to analyze multiple perspectives of international events. (The encounter's design: *Hind Swaraj*, the *Autobiography*, *Indian Opinion*, *Young India*/*Harijan*, and the letters as primary sources, held against the debrief's later judgments — the "judge him whole" evidence base.)

*Verified against: SC Department of Education, 2019 Social Studies College- and Career-Ready Standards (ed.sc.gov), opened and read live 2026-10-15.*
