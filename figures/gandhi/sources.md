## Authored devices

Dramatic devices the encounter uses that the history does not document. Plausible is not documented — say which.

- **The setting** (Birla House, January 1948, the Delhi fast just ended, receiving visitors) — a dramatic device; no specific documented conversation. Historically plausible, not documented.
- **Stall lines, suggestion pools, nudges** — pure engagement machinery from the scaffold, not history.
- **The closing monologue** — scripted; the sentiments are documented, the occasion is invented.
- **The early-writings candor instruction** ("do not soften, do not explain away, do not invent a retraction") — an authorial design decision: the figure's refusal to perform a modern apology is the evidence the student judges. The prompt names the failure where the figure's words fail.

## Historical caveats

Things a teacher may be asked about, with the honest answer.

- **He never publicly retracted the early words.** To the author's knowledge, the record contains no public retraction or apology for the early South African writings on Black Africans. What the record does show is a decisive shift in substance: the Zulu episode reframed in the *Autobiography* ("no war but a man hunt"), the 1908 "commingle" passage, the 1942 Roosevelt letter naming Africa's exploitation, and a lifetime of work against discrimination of all kinds. Guha's judgment ("he outgrew his racism quite decisively") and Desai & Vahed's caution (against the reinvented saint) are both noted; the encounter does not adjudicate the historiography — it presents the record and hands the student the judgment.
- **The period slur is never reproduced.** The encounter paraphrases the substance of what he wrote ("the white man's word for them"; "troublesome, very dirty, and live like animals" — the 1908 *Indian Opinion* line, paraphrased). The teacher should know the word itself is in the corpus.
- **The 1908 "commingle" line is cited via secondary sources** ("If we look into the future, is it not a heritage we have to leave to posterity, that all the different races commingle and produce a civilization that perhaps the world has not yet seen?"). The teacher should treat it as secondary-sourced, not as a verified primary quotation.
- **"My life is my message" is documented.** He wrote it in Bengali — *Amar Jeevan-i Amar Vani* — as his farewell message at Calcutta, signed "Mo Ka Gandhi," before leaving for Delhi on September 7, 1947.
- **Famous misattributions to keep out of the voice:** "Be the change you wish to see" (the documented form is "We but mirror the world..."); "An eye for an eye only ends up making the whole world blind" (no source in the corpus); "First they ignore you..." (no source). The voice never uses any of these.
- **Salt March figures:** 78 volunteers, ~240 miles (385 km), 24 days (March 12–April 6, 1930); the salt law broken at 8:30 a.m. on April 6; Gandhi arrested the night of May 4–5; over 60,000 jailed in the months after; the Dharasana satyagraha followed on May 21, led by Sarojini Naidu and reported by the American journalist Webb Miller.
- **The fasts:** Calcutta, begun 8:15 p.m. September 1, 1947, broken 9:15 p.m. September 4 (73 hours), at the Beliaghata house (Hydari Mansion), after a signed pledge from Hindu and Muslim representatives; Delhi, begun January 13, 1948, broken January 18, after a multi-community pledge to protect the Muslim minority.
- **The attempts he knew:** June 25, 1934, Poona (bomb at his motorcade); July 1944, Panchgani (a young man — Nathuram Godse, per secondary accounts — with a dagger; Gandhi invited him to stay and talk); June 1946, the train sabotage attempt (boulders on the track between Nerul and Karjat). His documented words after the 1946 attempt: "By the grace of God I have escaped from the jaws of death seven times... I aim to live till the age of 125." He knew nothing of the January 20, 1948 bomb or of January 30.
- **The assassination:** January 30, 1948, ~5:17 p.m., on the way to the evening prayer meeting at Birla House; three shots from a Beretta M1934; he died soon after in his room. Godse was tried, sentenced to death November 8, 1949, and hanged at Ambala Central Jail on November 15, 1949; Gandhi's sons Manilal and Ramdas had pleaded for commutation. The RSS was briefly banned after the assassination; the ban was lifted in 1949.
- **Partition:** he opposed it to the end and refused to be in Delhi for the August 15 celebrations, staying in riot-torn Calcutta instead. The "vivisection" line often attributed to him on partition was not verified in the corpus for this build and is not used in the voice.
- **Noakhali:** November 1946–February 1947, walking village to village alone after the massacres — documented; the voice references it in the "limits" answer.
- **Mountbatten's "one-man boundary force"** tribute is posthumous (after his death) and belongs to the debrief/teacher, never the voice.
