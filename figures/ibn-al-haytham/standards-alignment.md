# Meet Ibn al-Haytham — Standards Alignment (verified 2026-10-15)

Encounter: students converse with Ibn al-Haytham (c. 965–c. 1040) about the *Kitab al-Manazir* (Book of Optics), his darkened-room trials, and his method — suspect the ancients, suspect yourself, submit only to demonstration. At the end each student renders the closing judgment: "He wrote that truth is found through doubt and experiment, not trust in the ancients. Judge the seeker."

**Grade band fit for every framework below: 9–12**, except where noted.

---

## 1. NGSS — light/optics and the nature of science

- **HS-PS4-3** — Evaluate the claims, evidence, and reasoning behind the idea that electromagnetic radiation can be described either by a wave model or a particle model, and that for some situations one model is more useful than the other. (Clarification statement: emphasis is on how the experimental evidence supports the claim and how a theory is generally modified in light of new evidence.) The intromission-vs-extramission debate is a live historical instance of this: two models of how vision works, settled by trial.
- **SEP 3 — Planning and Carrying Out Investigations (9–12)** — Plan an investigation or test a design individually and collaboratively to produce data to serve as the basis for evidence as part of building and revising models. His aperture trials (varying hole size and shape, reading the eclipse image) are the pattern; the debrief's "darken a room and try it" hands the trial to the student.
- **Nature of science, Appendix H — "Science is a Human Endeavor"** — Science is a result of human endeavors, imagination, and creativity. The encounter's debrief makes this explicit: the experimental method has a non-European origin story, and the "Bacon invented it" map needs correcting.

Below-band but content-relevant (flagged honestly): **4-PS4-2** ("Develop a model to describe that light reflecting from objects and entering the eye allows objects to be seen") states his intromission finding almost verbatim, and **MS-PS4-2** ("waves are reflected, absorbed, or transmitted through various materials") matches the darkened-room trial — but both sit below the encounter's band.

*Verified against: NGSS performance-expectation texts via keslerscience.com 3D one-pagers, see.isbscience.org, and the teachingscientificliteracy.com practice summaries.*

**Correction to the library's provisional note:** the teacher-instructions draft suggested "NGSS HS-PS4-2 (wave behavior of light)." HS-PS4-2 is *not* a light standard — it covers the advantages of digital transmission and storage of information. Do not use it here.

## Verification caveats

1. **C3 Framework**: indicator texts verified against three independent curriculum documents quoting the Framework verbatim (they agree with each other); the official NCSS PDF was not directly retrievable. Official publisher: NCSS (2013), socialstudies.org/c3.
2. **NGSS**: HS-PS4-3 is the only in-band (9–12) physics fit; the ray-model details of his optics fall under geometric optics, which NGSS treats qualitatively at this band — the fit is through "models of light evaluated against evidence," not through any geometric-optics performance expectation (NGSS has none at HS).
3. **Grade-band mismatches**: CA (Grade 7) and SC (Grade 6) place Islamic Golden Age content below the encounter's 9–12 band. NY (Grade 10), FL (9–12), and TX (high school) are in-band.
4. **Texas**: high-school Physics TEKS were not verified this session; no physics code is cited.
